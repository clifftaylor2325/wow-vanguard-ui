# Look and theme

What Vanguard does here, in full. This page is part of the behaviour spec; the [README](../../README.md) has the overview and [docs/README.md](../README.md) lists every page.

## Look and layout

The Slate & Brass theme: flat slate panels (floating windows get a soft shadow; frames on the HUD stay flat unless you turn on Soft shadows under frames), a brass accent (or arcane, jade, orchid, copper; the older names fel, void and ember still work, and a profile that picked one keeps its colour), state colors kept off the red-green axis, a Safer colours switch under General → Look that turns friendly, neutral and hostile into blue, yellow and vermilion and gives the four dispel types (Magic, Curse, Disease, Poison) colours that stay apart under red-green and blue-yellow colour blindness (after a reload; it also comes on by itself while the game's own colour-blind mode is on, unless you untick Follow the game's colour-blind mode), bundled Fira Sans Condensed, pixel-perfect borders, smooth bars (Vanguard, Soft or Flat bar texture), shared fonts and bar textures (the Font picker under General lists Vanguard's two fonts and the one you use if it comes from another addon, the Bar texture picker also lists every bar texture other addons share through LibSharedMedia, and Vanguard shares its own font and bars the same way; if the addon behind your pick is gone, Fira Sans Condensed and the Vanguard bars come back), soft sparks on cast and power bars, buttons that ease into a lighter fill under the mouse, go a shade darker with their label a pixel lower while pressed, and grey their label when they can't be used, windows that rise a few pixels into place as they open (at once in combat), quiet fades (never longer than 0.15 s in combat) and interface sounds (Reduce motion and Interface sounds switches turn them off; every fade and sound is listed in `docs/THEME.md`). Vanguard's own windows share one look. The options window, setup, What's new, the quest log, the group board, the unlock popover and every dialog (export and import, questions, the reload offer, feedback, the wizards, the auction filters) have a title at the top left in the body text colour (setup and What's new centre theirs), a close button in the top-right corner and Escape to close them (the quest dialog has no close button: Goodbye and Escape end the talk). Escape also closes the meter's spell panel and the Instance prep card, and the panels Vanguard puts beside a game window (the mail, auction and crafting helpers, the talent plan) close with that window. It leaves alone what stays up while you play (the bars, unit and raid frames, the meter, trackers, strips, the raid tools and the officer roster), the unlock bar (Save and lock or Exit without saving decide what happens to your moves), loot rolls and Vanguard's AFK screen (any key ends it). It works the game's way, so it never takes keys from movement or casting, in combat too. In dialogs and the setup wizard Tab and Shift-Tab move a focus ring along the buttons, and Enter or Space presses the one it is on (out of combat); in the options window Left and Right step a focused slider, a dropdown keeps its lit fill while its menu is open, and Escape, a click outside or closing the window it came from (a bag, the auction house) closes the menu; an edit box's border turns the accent colour while you type, a search box shows a clear glyph while it has text, and a pressed switch drops its knob a pixel without changing colour. Where a window scrolls (the options pages, What's new, the quest log and quest dialog, the text and lists in the dialogs), the scroll thumb is one thin line with soft edges and no arrows, while the auction tables, the dropdown menus and the click-cast spell picker scroll with the mouse wheel alone; section headings are small grey capitals; a chosen list row has an accent bar along its left edge over a lighter fill, and a row presses a shade darker under the click; and a dialog has at most one accent button, on the action it exists for (Import, Export, Reload, Save, Make report), while Yes, No, Cancel and Later stay plain (the accent button has a bold dark label and eases to a lighter accent under the mouse; a button that deletes keeps the plain look with a red label). Dropdowns and sliders share one 200-wide control column on the right of an options row, and a long caption wraps to its left. Every frame can be moved in unlock mode (`/vg unlock`), each shown as a handle with its name inside it (a long name wraps onto a second line; the selected handle adds its point and offsets on a second line, on the name's line for a thin bar, and nothing for a handle under 120 wide, since the popover shows the numbers), with a grid (its lines one screen pixel thick at any UI scale), snapping and arrow-key nudging; a bar at the top of the screen says how ("Drag to move · right-click for anchor, scale, opacity · arrows nudge (Shift ×10)"; on a small screen or at a large text size its note wraps onto a second line instead of running off the screen) and offers **Save and lock** (the same as `/vg lock`) and **Exit without saving**, which puts every frame back as it was when you unlocked (spot, links, size, scale and opacity) and locks; when you unlocked from **Move frames** in the options window, either button opens it again on the same page (a fight or `/vg lock` just locks) (when the game's Edit Mode shows the handles there is no bar: closing Edit Mode keeps your changes). Right-click a frame's handle to set its anchor, exact offsets, scale (50% to 200%) and opacity (10% to 100%), or pick a frame under General → Layout and use its Scale and Opacity sliders. The popover (320 wide, so long German and Russian captions stay clear of its dropdowns at any text size) opens just right of the handle, its title says which frame it is and where it is anchored, and it closes with Done, its close button or Escape. A scaled frame grows around its anchor point, which stays where it was; a frame that fades by itself (a mouseover bar, say) still fades, at its opacity. A frame can also be anchored to another frame instead of the screen: in its popover pick **Anchor to** (a frame) and a **Side** (left, right, top or bottom) with typed X and Y offsets from it; it then follows that frame when it moves, is reset, rescaled or resized, or when the screen size or UI scale changes (out of combat), grows away from it (anchored to its right, it grows to the right), keeps the link when you drag or nudge it (the offsets change), and while that frame isn't there (its module off) sits at its last spot, never off screen. The cast bars (width) and the XP and reputation bars (width and height) can match another frame's width or height (**Match width**, **Match height**), kept as that frame changes, or take a size of their own from the Its width and Its height sliders under General → Layout; a matched slider is locked, and hovering it says what it matches and how to unmatch. A link that can't be (to the frame itself, round in a circle, a size the frame can't be, or a frame Vanguard doesn't size) is refused, and the popover says why (it grows taller to hold the line, above Reset and Done). Scale, opacity, links and sizes are saved with the frame's position, go with the profile (export, import, switching), and `/vg reset` puts them back (100%, on the screen, its own size). The game's Edit Mode shows Vanguard's handles too, under its own window (which stays on top) with a note below it (it grows to hold its text at any text size and language), so you can move everything in one place (a switch under General turns that off). Vanguard's frames replace the game's, so Edit Mode shows no handle for a game frame Vanguard has hidden (the game's action bars, XP and reputation bars, unit, boss and cast bar frames, the quest tracker while Vanguard's is on, and the rest); it keeps every system Vanguard doesn't replace. Profiles can be exported and imported, and can switch with your spec (on clients that have specializations). An export holds the whole profile or only the parts you pick: **Choose what to export** on the Profiles page (or `/vg export pick`) has a switch for Layout (positions and movers), Situations overrides, Global settings (theme, modules list, skins) and each module with saved settings, and `/vg export layout` and the like name parts in chat. A partial string imports into the profile you use, its values replacing yours key by key while every setting it doesn't carry keeps your value (a frame's spot and a situation's override are replaced whole). Whole or partial, an imported string never brings its Pixel-perfect scale switch or multiple: those belong to the screen it was made on, so yours stay. Every string says its format and the Vanguard version that made it, and a string from an older version (or from ForeverUI) is upgraded as it imports; a large one imports a little each frame, with how far it has got ("Importing… 40%") in the import box's title, and one Vanguard can't use says why. Before any import, the profile you use is copied into one backup profile, `<name> backup` (each import replaces it, unless another character or one of your specs uses that profile: then the backup goes to `<name> backup 2`, or the next free number), and chat says so; a font or bar texture the string names that this client doesn't have is named in chat, with what Vanguard draws instead. A whole profile whose settings match one you have (your own export, say) switches to that one instead of making a copy, and says so. A whole-profile string also carries the UI scale and screen size it was made on: importing one asks whether to use its UI scale (the game's own UI scale setting, applied out of combat), then offers the reload. A profile remembers which of the game's Edit Mode layouts you use with it (that goes in its string too): when a character loads a different profile than last time (a switch, a spec profile or an import), Vanguard switches Edit Mode to that layout, out of combat, if your account has one of that name, and says so in chat; characters sharing a profile keep their own layouts. Choosing a profile (the list under `/vg` → Profiles, the picker at the top of the window, `/vg profile <name>`, an imported whole profile or a spec change) takes effect when you reload: until then the frames on screen still belong to the profile you were using, so moving a frame or changing the accent changes that one, and the Profiles page says which profile comes next (**Use** on the profile in use takes the switch back).

## Centered layout

Everything near the action is anchored to the bottom center of the screen, so the layout keeps its shape at any UI scale. Player and target sit either side of your character with your cast bar and swing timer between them, the target of target outside the target, the pet over the player frame's right end (the chat fills the room left of the player frame), the focus above the player, and three bars of 38 px buttons under them over thin XP and reputation bars. A party or a raid of 10 grows up from just above your player frame, centered on it. A bigger raid (25 or 40) moves to the left of your character, as healers usually keep it, so the boss and your own frames stay in view, and stacks its groups as rows (five across, one group above the next): its left edge is 500 units left of the screen's middle and its bottom 344 up, clear of your pet and focus and, on a screen as narrow as 1365 UI units, of the roll tracker at the left edge. A raid of more than 10 is also drawn smaller (80%, or 72% above 25) so all 40 fit. **Stack big raids at the left (more than 10 players)** on the Raid frames page in `/vg` turns that off (a big raid then stays over your player frame, in columns). Frames you moved keep their spot at every group size; only the defaults changed. `/vg reset raid` forgets a saved spot (the one setup's Healing layout used to save, or one you dragged to) and puts the raid frames at the default, the grid following at once. Chat starts in a bottom-left spot under the frames, sized once; move it with `/vg unlock` (the game's own layout and your resizing are left alone after that), or switch the spot off under Chat. A test checks that nothing overlaps or leaves the screen, solo to 40-man, at UI scale 1.0 and pixel-perfect, and with each of setup's screen presets on the screen it is made for. Frames you moved keep their spot; `/vg reset all` puts everything back.

## Text size

One slider (/vg > General, 70% to 130%) sizes all of Vanguard's text, chat and tooltips included, and applies at once. Chat has its own Small, Medium or Large on top (or keeps the game's own chat size if you prefer). `/vg status` shows your UI scale and how many pixels tall chat text draws on your screen.

## Theme variants

Theme variants (General → Look → Theme variant; `/vg variant`): Slate and brass is the default. High contrast uses opaque near-black panels, white text, no sheen or gradient, full-saturation state colors, Safer colours on and a 2-pixel accent ring on accent-bordered frames. Vellum and ink is a warm dark with cream text. A variant swaps surface and text colors (it wins over a saved panel colour), keeps your accent, and is saved in the profile and its export string. Classic look is dark leather and bronze with gold-cream text, a bronze border, the window ornaments always on and, unless you set **Minimap shape** (Map → Minimap and waypoints; reload) to Square, a round minimap (its zone name on a strip above the disc, the corner buttons inside its outline) and a flat bronze-edged backing under each action bar unless you set **Bar backing** (Action bars page) to Never (all drawn by Vanguard; nothing from the game's art). Midnight is a cold navy-black with ice-blue text, Obsidian a neutral charcoal with light grey text and a silver edge, Moss a dark forest green with pale sage text, and Ash and ember a smoky grey with a rust edge and warm grey text.

## Panel texture

Panel texture (General → Look → Panel texture): Flat is the default. A texture is drawn over the fill of every floating window (the options window, dialogs, menus, tooltips, bags, the quest log and the game windows Vanguard skins), never on the HUD, and it changes at once. Vanguard's own Fine grain, Carved stone and Dark leather lighten the panel by a few shades, so they suit every theme variant and keep text readable. Rock, Marble and Dialog are the game's own backgrounds, drawn faint over the panel colour (nothing from the game's art is copied into Vanguard). The list also offers every background another addon shares through LibSharedMedia, and Vanguard shares its three as "Vanguard Grain", "Vanguard Stone" and "Vanguard Leather". With rounded corners the texture stops short of the arcs; High contrast keeps windows flat whatever the pick. If the addon behind a shared pick is gone, windows draw flat, and an imported string that names one says so. The pick is saved in the profile and its export string.

## Corners

Corners (General → Look → Corners; `/vg corners`): Square is the default. Rounded gives every painted panel, HUD frame, tooltip and button a four-pixel rounded corner (the arc is one pixel wide, like the border; a button's hover and pressed fills, an accent button's fill and a chosen segment's fill follow it, so none pokes out past the arc) on any theme; status bars, slots and tabs stay square, and a rounded window skips the panel gradient. Saved in the profile.

## Shield corners

Shield corners (on by default; General → Look → Shield corners on unit frames): the player, target, target of target, focus, pet, party and boss frames have each corner cut at 45 degrees, eight pixels across, the shape of the Vanguard crest, with their health bar cut to match at the top and their power bar at the bottom; cast bars keep the theme's corners; with the switch off they follow the Corners setting like every other panel. Saved in the profile.

## Faction look

Faction look (crest only by default; General → Look → Faction look: Crest only, Crest and heading font, or Off): an Alliance character gets the crossed swords in gold; a Horde character gets the crossed axes in red. Crest and heading font also sets Cinzel (Alliance) or Metamorphous (Horde) for window and card titles. The crest sits just right of the title of the options window and of the setup wizard, on the AFK card, and faintly behind the top-right corner of the quest dialog and the quest log. Numbers, body text and the minimap's zone name stay in Fira Sans, the HUD and your accent color are unchanged, and neutral characters (and Korean and Chinese clients, which use the game's font) keep the usual look; Russian clients keep Fira Sans for titles and headings (the faction fonts have no Cyrillic) and get the crest. `/vg factionart` lists which of the game's own faction pictures your client has.

## AFK screen

While you're away (`/afk`, or the game marks you idle), the screen dims to a card with your name in your class color, level and class, the zone, how long you've been away (since the game marked you, a fight in between included), the time and your XP bar. Under the card, while the faction look is on, your faction's passage sits in its heading font and crest color: Varian's last letter to Anduin for the Alliance, the Blood Oath of the Horde for the Horde. Moving or a fight brings everything back; a click or a key puts it away until you're next away, and a ready check or any popup (an invite, a role check) puts it away at once so your first click lands on the prompt. It stays shut inside a dungeon, raid, battleground or arena, and while you cast or channel (it tries again once, 30 seconds later). It only shows out of combat and never hides the game's own frames. Turn it off under Modules.
