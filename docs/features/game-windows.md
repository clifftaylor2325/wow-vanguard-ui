# Game windows and extras

What Vanguard does here, in full. This page is part of the behaviour spec; the [README](../../README.md) has the overview and [docs/README.md](../README.md) lists every page.

## The game's extras, tidied

The game's own XP and reputation bars are hidden while either of Vanguard's two data bars is on (no more double XP bar; they share one container, so with only one of them on the other is not drawn by the game either, and both of the game's bars come back only when both are off), and so are the clock and calendar button while the info strip shows them (`/calendar` still opens the calendar, a calendar event's reminder still comes up in chat, and a click on the strip's time opens the game's clock window, or turns off its alarm while it rings) and the armoured-man durability figure whenever the info strip is on, its durability block or not (the strip's block and the character pane's gear labels carry the warning; switch the strip off to have the game's figure back). Tracking, mail, dungeon difficulty, the addon compartment and the group finder eye move onto the square minimap's corners (tracking and the addon compartment as slot buttons like the menu bar's, with Vanguard's icons, on the band along the map's bottom), and the expansion button (nothing to show in vanilla content) is put away; the game's emptied minimap frame at the top right stops taking mouse clicks and its zone button is put away. The bag buttons and the micro menu share one bar in the bottom-right corner: the bags (the key ring too, where the client has one, on the same slot as a bag; on retail the backpack and the bags, which the game draws at two sizes, all on the same slot, the backpack with the game's backpack icon, and the game's fold arrow kept between them) in a row centered over the menu buttons, with a thin accent line between them under the slots. The bar hugs its slots with a 12-pixel margin all round and grows to the left from its bottom-right corner as the game adds buttons. Every button sits on a slot 24 pixels wide with a 4-pixel gap (a slate tile with a beveled edge and a small soft shadow; the menu row's slots are 30 tall, the game's menu plates' own shape, so each plate fits its slot whole) and keeps the game's own icon and alert flash, fitted inside the slot; an inner edge in your accent shows under the mouse and darkens while you press, and a disabled menu button turns grey. Each bag's slot has a thin bar along its bottom showing how full that bag is (red when it is full; a soul bag or quiver counts only its own slots), an empty bag slot shows the game's empty-slot art faint and grey, and the backpack shows how many slots your bags have free in its corner, in place of the game's own count (amber at 4 or fewer, red at none, the same rule as the info strip's "free"). A bag's tooltip adds how many of its own slots are free ("9 of 16 free"). Vanguard's line icons stand in for the game's menu icons (on by default; the switch under Bars and bags brings the game's own back, and a profile saved before the bar was reworked keeps the game's icons until you switch). The bar is always shown; you can choose On mouseover, Auto-hide after 10 s (shown when you log in and while the mouse is over it, faded 10 seconds after the mouse leaves), In combat, Hidden or the same row of conditions as the action bars instead, under Bars and bags; it applies at once. The game's own bar frames are hidden inside it, and a spot you gave the menu strip is kept (a separate bag strip's spot is dropped, since the bags now ride in the bar). The quest tracker has its spot under the info strip: Vanguard's own tracker fills it, and only with that switched off does the game's tracker sit there instead, with flat headers, a brass line under each, the theme's collapse arrows and the theme font (quest colors stay the game's). The bar sizes itself to its buttons, re-fits whenever the game lays the bars out again, and stays where it's put when the game lays out its own frames. Each has a switch under Bars and bags.

## Game windows

The game's own windows keep their layout and buttons and get Vanguard's flat panel (opaque: the world doesn't show through), close button, title (every window's and dialog's title in the same size and light text, where the game centres it) and buttons; the sunk area behind a window's list or content (its inset) is a darker inset panel. Each group below has its own switch under Game windows (a reload applies it). The character pane, the combined bag, the group finder, the guild window, the spellbook and talents, the Legacy window, collections and appearances, the game menu, the auction house, friends, merchant, mail, professions, achievements, the clock and chat channels can be dragged by their title bar and open where you dropped them; those and the calendar, the inspect window, trade, the dress-up window, the trainer, the guild bank, macros, the stable, the raid window, letters, the loot history, books, the add-ons list, help, the game's settings, chat settings and guild control can also be dragged by an empty spot along their top edge (never one with a button on it), as can Vanguard's own options window; the world map, loot and roll windows, popups and the ready check stay where the game puts them. A dragged window stops short of the screen's edge, so its panel border and the tabs under it stay in view, and the character pane's mode tabs at its right side too. Nothing moves in combat: a window the game places during a fight goes back to your spot when the fight ends, and a drag you are still holding as a fight starts ends there, with the window kept where it was. The switch "Drag game windows by their title bar" (a reload applies it) and a "Reset window positions" button are under Game windows. Reset forgets every place and puts each window you dragged where the game puts it: an open one at once (a letter goes back beside the mailbox), a closed one the next time it opens, and the options window in the middle; in a fight the game's windows wait until it ends, and chat says so.

## The game's tools

The game's Cooldown Manager (turn it on under the game's Options, Gameplay Enhancements, or with Vanguard's switch "Show the game's manager" on the Cooldowns page, off until you tick it, which turns the game's setting on and, unticked, off again; Vanguard says so once if it's off) gets square icons in thin borders with the theme font, its cooldown timers in the theme's timer font, and its rows sit between the player and target frames, above the swing timer, the essential row nearest them. ## Quiet the game

Quiet the game (each off until you switch it on, but for the action-bar errors switch, under Game windows, "Quiet the game"): decline guild invites (the game's invite window closes), decline group invites from people who are not friends, Battle.net friends or guildmates (the invite stays when the game hides who it is from), decline Battle.net friend requests (each says in chat what it did, once per sender every 5 minutes, so someone who keeps asking doesn't fill your chat; friend requests carry no name, and senders the game hides count as one, so those say it once every 5 minutes), release your spirit at once when you die in a battleground (not an arena, and not when the game offers you a way to bring yourself back, such as a soulstone or Reincarnation), hide the red error line, but for four errors you must act on when the game sends them as errors: full bags, a full quest log, too many of an item and loot already gone, and never the yellow quest progress lines (or, on by default, only the errors the action bar already shows: not ready, not enough rage or energy, no target; after a reload), hide the zone and subzone names that fade in on entering an area (both apply at once), and a furthest camera zoom of 1.5, 2 or 2.6 times (the game's own setting stays until you pick one, and picking "The game's setting" again puts back the zoom you had before Vanguard changed it; set out of combat).

## Auto-accept

Auto-accept (each off until you switch it on, under Game windows, "Auto-accept"): accept summons (one offered in a fight is taken when the fight ends, if it is still waiting and you are alive; and only once the game would let you take it: while it would not, its prompt stays up), accept resurrections (the game's prompt closes whichever kind it was; while the game still makes you wait to come back, its prompt stays up for you to accept when it unlocks), decline duel requests (duels to the death too; each says in chat what it did, once per sender every 5 minutes), and faster auto-loot: when the game is auto-looting (its Auto Loot setting, or its auto-loot key held), every slot is taken at once instead of one by one; a slot the game has locked (an item still being rolled for) stays in the loot window, and a bind-on-pickup item still asks first.
