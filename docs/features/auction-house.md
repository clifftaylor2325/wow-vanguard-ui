# Auction house

What Vanguard does here, in full. This page is part of the behaviour spec; the [README](../../README.md) has the overview and [docs/README.md](../README.md) lists every page.

## Auction helper

At the auction house, two Vanguard tabs join the game's window after its Buy, Sell and Auctions: the **Lists** tab (the Lists pane on the left, with the scan button under it, and the list picked on the right) and the **Posting** tab (the Sell card on the left; what's up now and your auctions on the right); the window's title says which is open, and a listing or deal opened to buy switches to the game's Buy tab (Lists brings you back where you were). **Lists**: the pane lists your named shopping lists with how many items each holds, then Recent, Deals and Upgrades; click one to show it on the card beside the pane (**New** makes a list; **+ Add** on the card's toolbar takes an item link, an item ID or a name; up to 20 lists of 50 items, kept for the account). The pane's buttons (two to a row, in three rows, so a long translated label fits) also **Rename**, **Copy**, **Delete** (after asking) and **Export** the list shown (as text to copy, one item per line), and **Import** into it: paste item names, IDs or links one per line, or a shopping list string from Auctionator (terms between `^`, fields between `;`: Vanguard takes each term's name, in quotes for that exact name, and says how many other fields it ignored); what doesn't fit in 50 is left out and counted. The last 20 items you searched (newest first, kept for the account) are the **Recent** entry in the pane: search it, copy it into a list, export it or clear it (+ Add on it adds to Shopping); **Search list** searches each item in turn (one search at a time, at most one a second, so well under the game's limit; "12 of 50" says how far it is), and the list's table shows each item's cheapest price per unit (Unit price), how many are up (Up; "none up" when nothing is) and its max price when a filter sets one (Max). Click a row and its listings replace the list: every auction of that item (a commodity's or any other item's), with its price per unit, quantity and time left; click a column's heading to sort by it (again to turn the order round), scroll for more (further pages are asked for as you near the end, through the same one-at-a-time searches), and with the mouse over the listings Up and Down mark one (with the accent bar and lighter fill every chosen row has) and Enter opens it (other keys stay the game's). Click a listing (or Enter) and the game's own list shows that item, to buy there; **Back** returns to the list; right-click a row for its menu: **Filters...** opens a dialog (Save, Cancel, a close button; Escape closes it too) that sets exact name only, a level range and a quality (the game's search applies them) and a max price (dearer listings are left out; on an item ID only the max price counts), and **Remove** takes it off the list. A name finds the item of that name, else the cheapest whose name contains it (not with exact name only), never an item named otherwise; exact names export in quotes; the mouse wheel scrolls the rows. **Restock** (on the card's toolbar, whose buttons widen to their labels where a translation runs longer, also **Buy list** on the Instance prep card and `/vg ah restock`) makes the shopping list Restock from what you're short of: your class's reagents for your level (as Instance prep counts them) and every Keep-stocked item (`/vg stock`) you carry fewer of than its count, each with how many more you need (an item on both needs the larger amount; ammo, soul shards, food, bandages and potions are kinds of item, not one item, so they're not on it). Made again, it takes the new amounts and keeps the filters you set on its items. At the auction house it is searched at once (away from it, chat says what's on it): each row says "need 8" until searched, then the item's listings are read (one more search, through the same one-at-a-time queue) and the row says what the cheapest 8 come to, "8 for 1g 20s 0c" (your own listings and those over the max price left out; an item that isn't a commodity is bought a whole listing at a time, so it may say "10 for"), in orange when fewer are up. **Sell**: drop an item on the card (or click it in your bags, as you would for the game's own Sell tab; with Posting open the window stays on Posting) and it shows the cheapest price someone else asks now (your own listings left out), the lowest of the last 7 days from your scans and searches, the deposit for the next stack, and the price it will ask: 1 silver under the cheapest (or 1 copper, 1 %, or the same price; never under 1 silver for the silver rule; in whole silver where the game takes no copper), else under the 7-day low when nothing is up. Under it a form: **Quantity** (the dropped stack for a commodity, one item otherwise; **Max** is all you have), **Stack** size (it starts at vanilla's usual stack for the item's kind: 20 for cloth, leather, ore, bars, herbs and food and drink, 5 for potions, elixirs, flasks and scrolls, 1 for anything else; **Max** puts it all in one), the number of **Stacks**, a **Price** box that shows the suggestion and takes a price you type (as 1g 50s 25c, or a plain number of gold such as 2.5, as in the shop's max price; nothing else counts as a price (not 1e5 or 0x10), and nothing above 999999g 99s 99c; per unit or per stack, by the button beside it, a stack price that doesn't divide evenly rounding each unit up so the stack never posts for less than you typed; empty, or text that isn't a price, goes back to the suggestion), a **Bid** for items (a commodity takes none), and the auction length (the game's three; a new item starts at the last one you used, or at a fixed one under Auction length). **Post** puts up one stack per click (the game allows no more) and loads the next, from whichever bag slot holds it, with a "posted 3 of 10" line under the buttons (the item's sales history line under that, both stopping at the card's edge); **Skip** passes a stack, **Stop** ends it. Post, Stop, Skip and Max widen to their whole label where a translation runs longer than the English. Under it, a posting guide shows four steps (1 Item, 2 Price, 3 Post, 4 Listed, each after the one before it, so a longer translation never runs over the next; a step that would pass the card's right edge, at larger text, starts a second row, and the line under the steps moves down) with the one you're on lit, and a line saying what to do next or what is happening: drop an item, checking what it sells for, click Post to list stack 1 of 10, stack 1 sent and waiting for the auction house, finished. When Post is off the line says why, in the warning colour: no price found, the bid not under the buyout, under what a vendor pays, not in your bags, or not enough money for the deposit. Post waits while the game is busy. When the game asks to confirm a price, Post becomes **Confirm** and the next click confirms it (one call per click, as the game requires); change the price, bid, quantity, stack or length first and it is Post again, so the next click posts what the card shows now. Each click says in chat what it sends ("Post: sending stack 1 of 10 (10 at 10c each, 24 Hours).") and, for 3 seconds after, what the game answers: its red error line, a post refused or dropped, a post it wants confirmed, or a call it blocked; the guide shows that answer until the next click. A bid goes up in whole silver where the game takes no copper, as the price does. **Up now**, the card at the top right, lists what is up for the item on the sell card: each listing's price each, how many and the time left, cheapest first, yours flagged with an accent square; the mouse wheel scrolls them, and near the end the next page is asked for (through the same one-search-at-a-time queue). Click a listing to take its price, less your undercut rule (your own listing's price as it is). When the price per unit is under what a vendor pays for the item, the price line turns red and says "Under vendor price", and Post stays off (unless you switch off "Never post under vendor price"). Each card (the Lists pane, the list shown, the scan, the sell card, Up now and Your auctions) sits in its own bordered box, in the look of the options window's cards, and its rows fill the room its box has. **Your auctions**, the card at the bottom right: **Check undercuts** asks for your auctions, checks each item's price now, and marks with a red square each auction someone else asks less for per unit; each row has a **Cancel** button (hover it for what cancelling costs), and only a click on it cancels. The heading then counts your auctions and says how many are undercut ("Your auctions 12 · 3 undercut", or "12 · none undercut"; the count takes the room the buttons leave, shrinking and ending in "..." in a long language), and **Cancel next undercut** cancels the first of them on each click, one auction per click, and moves to the next (an auction whose cancel the game drops or refuses is offered again after 5 seconds) (hover it for which one and what cancelling costs; **Check undercuts** and **Cancel next undercut** are each as wide as their labels); with none left it says so and stops. A mark stays until the next check. Each row also says how the auction stands: still up (your price per unit, with Cancel), sold ("sold · 7s 50c", what it sold for, in green), or expired ("expired · by mail" while the item comes back by mail); once the item is back in your bags it says "expired · in bags", and its **Relist** button puts the item on the sell card, its price checked as if you had dropped it there. The rows stay while the auction house is closed, so an expired one is still there when you come back from the mailbox. If you like (off until you switch it on), your auctions are checked again after each post and each cancel, one search at a time like any other. **Scan the auction house** (one scan every 15 minutes for the whole account, the game's own limit, counted from the start of a scan that finished: one cut short by closing the auction house doesn't count; until then the button, as wide as its card, is greyed and reads "Next scan in 12 min", and the line under it reads "Scanned 2 min ago · 8,412 items" for this realm and faction, in the warning colour once that is over a day old; when the game doesn't answer a scan within 90 seconds, as it may when another character or auction addon scanned in the last 15 minutes, the scan is given up, chat says why, the button counts down 15 minutes from the try, and a list that still comes is read) reads every listing, a few hundred per frame so the game never stalls, and records, leaving your own listings out (the chat line says how many were yours), each item's cheapest price per unit, how many listings and units are up, and its market value (the average price of the cheapest 15 % of the units, so one cheap listing doesn't set it and a few dear ones don't lift it); from then on item tooltips everywhere say "AH: 1s 0c · market 1s 50c (today)" (today, yesterday or so many days ago by your computer's clock) (the market left out when it's the cheapest price), and over a stack a second line says what the stack comes to at both ("AH x20: 20s 0c · market 30s 0c"). Searches feed the prices between scans: the shop's list searches, the game's own browse list, the sell card's price check and Check undercuts each record the cheapest price someone else asks (your own listings left out; from the browse list only for items it prices per unit, commodities and equipment), while the units up and the market value come only from a full scan. Prices are kept per realm and faction, since each has its own auction house (every character of yours on that realm and side shares them; prices saved by an older version move once to the realm and faction you next log in with). Where the game names a ruleset (a season, or Hardcore rules), it joins the realm and faction, and what was kept for the realm and faction moves to it once; `/vg realm` shows the key in use, with each day's low, units up and market value for 7 days; one not seen for 30 days is forgotten, and so is a realm and faction left with none. Shopping lists are kept for the whole account. `/vg ah` says how many items are priced on this realm and faction and when you last scanned there, `/vg ah list <name>` picks or makes a list and `/vg ah add <list> <item>` adds to one (`/vg ah add <item>`, a link or an ID with no list named, to the list in use), `/vg ah rename <name>`, `duplicate`, `delete`, `export` and `import` do the same as the pane's buttons for the list shown, `/vg ah recent` shows Recent, `/vg ah restock` makes (and at the auction house searches) the Restock list, `/vg ah scan` scans and `/vg ah prices clear` forgets them (on every realm). Under Town and gear (Auction house): the tabs, the tooltip line and the scan button each have a switch, the undercut rule and the auction length a new item starts with (Last used, or one of the game's three) are there too (and on the card), and so are "Never post under vendor price" (on) and checking your auctions again after each post and cancel (off). Buying and bidding stay in the game's own window.

## Deals

At the auction house, the Deals entry in the Lists tab's pane lists what your scans and searches found priced under an item's usual price: under what a vendor pays for it ("< vendor": buy it and sell it to a merchant), under 70 % of its market value over the last 7 days (the middle of each day's market value from your scans, so one odd day doesn't skew it, and only once scans have priced it on at least two of those days; "-35 %" says how far under), or, for an item without that,  under its lowest price on the six days before today. The best come first (under vendor price first, then the furthest under); each row shows the item, its unit price, how far under it is and what it was judged against (hovering it says the same), the pane's count and "12 found" count them, and the mouse wheel scrolls them. Click one and the game's own list shows that item, to buy there; nothing is bought for you. Each search judges its item again, so a deal bought or gone dearer drops off. Deals are kept for 30 minutes after the auction house closes, through a trip to the mailbox, back at the same realm and faction's auction house (they are forgotten after that, whether or not you come back): the heading then reads "10 found, the oldest 12 min ago" and each row's tooltip says when it was found. A switch under Town and gear (Auction house) turns the entry off.

## Upgrades

At the auction house, the Upgrades entry in the Lists tab's pane lists the armor and weapons your scans and item searches saw that beat what you wear, judged by Gear upgrades' stat weights for your class and role (the same judgement as the tooltip's "Upgrade +12% (estimate)" line: rings and trinkets against the weaker you wear, a two-hander against both hands, gear you can't wear left alone). A scan reads each gear listing's link (gear alone, and judged a hundred listings a frame after the scan, so the game never stalls) and an item search's results carry theirs; the game's browse list carries none, so it feeds nothing here. Every listing that gains at least half a per cent is a row, the biggest gain first (the cheapest among equals; the same item seen twice keeps its cheapest listing); for an empty slot only the best listing (the highest score, the cheapest among equals) is kept, so white gear never floods the list. Each row shows the item, its slot, the gain ("+12 %", or "empty slot") and its price per unit; its tooltip is the item's own, with the Upgrade line; the pane's count and "7 found" count them. Click one and the game's own list shows that item, to buy there; nothing is bought for you. The listings are judged again when what you wear or the weights change. Like Deals they are kept for 30 minutes after the auction house closes (the heading and each tooltip say how long ago they were found), back at the same realm and faction's auction house, and forgotten when what you wear changes while the auction house is closed. A switch under Town and gear (Auction house) turns the entry off; with Gear upgrades off the entry says so and judges nothing.

## Price alerts

A shopping list item with a max price (right-click its row, **Filters...**) can alert you at that price: tick "Alert at or under the max price". Whenever a scan or a search at the auction house (the shop's list searches, the game's own browse list, the sell card's price check, Check undercuts) sees that item up at or under its max price (for a name, the items the shop would find for it: that name, or one containing it unless exact, of its quality and level range), the alert sound plays and the Chat watch toast says "Price alert", the item, its max price and the price seen; several at once (a whole scan) share one toast, "3 price alerts". Click the toast and the game's own list shows the item, to buy there; right-click dismisses it. An item alerts once a session, and again only when it is seen cheaper than when it last alerted. Alerts come from your named lists, not Recent; without a max price there is nothing to alert at, so the switch is not kept. The sound follows Interface sounds (not Chat watch's Sound switch), and the toast works with Chat watch off.

## Disenchant values

Weapons and armour of green quality or better get a tooltip line under the auction price, "Disenchant: ~1g 20s": what disenchanting it is worth on average, from vanilla's disenchanting tables (the dust, essences, shards and crystals each quality and item level gives, how often and how many) and each material's market value (else its cheapest price) from your scans and searches on this realm and faction. Gear whose materials have no price yet gets no line. It is on for characters who know Enchanting (read from your professions, or from your skills where the client has no profession list) and off for the rest until you choose, under Town and gear (Auction house); setting it back to what your professions give (its reset dot, say) is no choice, so it follows Enchanting again. The tables are written from vanilla's and still to be checked in the game.

## Sales ledger

The auction helper keeps a record of what became of your auctions: each sale (the item, how many, what it went for, when), each auction that ran out and each you cancelled, for this realm and faction (and ruleset, as for prices; kept 90 days, at most 2000 records per realm and faction; forgetting the prices with `/vg ah prices clear` keeps it). It learns of them from the auction house's own messages while you play (a sale or expiry notice, your auctions list, a cancel) and from your mailbox: each "Auction successful" invoice and each "Auction expired" mail is read when the inbox shows (nothing is opened, taken or deleted). A sale the game told you of and its mail later are one sale, counted once, with the mail's figures (what the buyer paid for how many); a mail read again is not counted again. On the sell card, **Your history** (top right) swaps the listings for the item's history: "sold 5 of 8 · avg 12s 50c" (how many of its auctions sold, of those that sold or ran out, and the average price each) and its last four records (the price each, or expired or cancelled; how many; when); **Up now** brings the listings back. The info strip can show this session's sales (Map → Strip under the minimap).

## Crafting costs

With a recipe open in the profession window, a Vanguard panel to its right (past the window's side tabs; to its left when the screen has no room at the right but has at the left, for a window you moved toward the right) says what the recipe costs and earns at the auction house: each reagent with how many and what they cost (at its market value from your scans and searches on this realm and faction, else its cheapest price; "no price" when none was seen), what the reagents come to ("at least" while one has no price), what the crafted item sells for (times the average number made; an enchant makes nothing to sell) and the profit, in green, or in red for a loss, before the auction house's cut (shown only once every price is known). Type how many crafts beside **Add reagents** and click it: each reagent joins your Keep-stocked list (`/vg stock`) with what that many crafts need (a larger count you already keep stays), so a merchant who sells it tops you up, and the Restock shopping list is made of what you're short of (searched at once at the auction house). Optional and finishing reagents are left out. A switch under Town and gear (Auction house) turns the panel off. In the auction tables each column is at least as wide as its heading.

## Auction prices for other addons

`Vanguard.API.v1` gives other addons this realm and faction's auction prices, vendor prices and disenchant values, tells them when new prices come in, makes and changes shopping lists, and searches the auction house through the Shop card (one search at a time, only while it is open), with the same function names as Auctionator's API so an addon written for it can switch easily. It never posts, buys or cancels. Addon authors: see [docs/API.md](../API.md).
