# Unit frames and nameplates

What Vanguard does here, in full. This page is part of the behaviour spec; the [README](../../README.md) has the overview and [docs/README.md](../README.md) lists every page.

## Unit frames

Player, target, target of target, focus and party frames (built when the raid frames don't cover your party; each shows while its party member exists and hides in a raid, where the raid frames show your group), and boss frames (up to five, in a column from the top-left corner, each shown while its boss is in the fight; the game's own boss frames are hidden). The player and target frames are 240 x 36 with 12 px names, sized for a 1080p screen at setup's 80% UI scale. On WoW Forever they show levels (colored by difficulty) and health as a percent, and a diamond marks elite, rare and boss targets (the health text ends just left of it on the target, focus and boss frames). Hovering any of them shows its unit's tooltip, as the game's own frames do (in a fight too, unless Tooltips' **Hide unit tooltips in combat** is on). A creature another player or group has tagged has a grey health bar, as on the game's frames (never a player or a player's pet). A released player reads Ghost, an offline or dead unit's health bar goes dark (as on the raid tiles) instead of keeping its class colour, and its health text reads Dead and Ghost in the warning-red state colour and Offline in the secondary text colour (as on the raid tiles; kept when the Versus deck changes the text), and a hunter in your group feigning death doesn't read Dead. A raid target icon sits centred on the top edge of each frame whose unit carries one (on the 24 tall target of target and pet frames, lifted just enough that its bottom clears the name; the boss frames, which stack close together, keep it centred so a mark never covers the frame above), following the **Raid target icons** switch on the Raid frames page; a mark the client hides shows nothing. A small mark by the diamond shows who has tagged the target: a green check when you have (a creature you can attack that is in combat and that the game does not call tapped by someone else; never a player or a player's pet or demon, which can't be tagged; the game has no call for "tagged by me" itself, so a creature fighting only your pet reads the same), a grey X when another player has (the game calls it tapped). Switched off under `/vg` → Unit frames. An **in-combat mark** (on until you untick it under `/vg` → Unit frames, applied at once): from the moment a fight starts until it ends, small vermilion crossed swords sit on your player frame's top-right corner (your health text always ends just left of that corner, so it never moves) and the frame's edge turns vermilion; your target's frame takes the same edge while the game says the target is in combat (an answer the client hides counts as not). **Calm HUD** (off until you turn it on; see [Look and theme](look-and-layout.md#calm-hud), which has the whole HUD): out of combat your player, pet, target, target of target and focus frames dim to a third, and they come back at once in a fight, while you target a living hostile unit, or under the mouse; only their opacity changes, so they stay clickable, and the client switches them in combat too. **Versus deck** (off until you turn it on under Unit frames, or pick the Versus deck layout in setup): one rounded panel with an accent border joins your player and target frames (and rounds their corners too, bars included, while it is on, instead of the shield cut) across the middle (under your cast bar, swing timer and cooldowns, which stay where they are), with the Vanguard crest centred on its top edge, both frames' names and health in the larger title text, and your target's health and power fill from the right, so the pair reads as a duel; it grows with a taller target frame (a damage or tank layout) so the border stays clear of it, and to wrap the rows your class adds to the player frame (combo points, totems, a druid's mana bar, the status chip when it sits above the frame; once a row is built the panel keeps its room), dims with the Calm HUD, and follows the two frames wherever you move them. Combo points for rogues and druids above the player frame, filled in the bar texture (five on Forever; on retail as many as your talents give, up to seven, a charged point lit in its own blue; they follow your points to a new target at once, as the game moves them without a power change). Above it (clear of the chat window's tab bar and its Find and Copy tiles), shamans get their totems (icon and time left; right-click one to destroy it; out of combat an empty slot lets clicks through to the world); under it, druids get their mana while bear or cat form shows rage or energy (on retail also Balance druids, Shadow priests and Elemental shamans, whose power bar shows another power): the game keeps both inside its own player frame, which Vanguard replaces. On retail the class resources the game's player frame draws come back too, as a row of pips above the frame: a paladin's holy power, a warlock's soul shards (from level 10, as the game shows them; by tenths for Destruction), a Windwalker's chi, an Arcane mage's charges, an Evoker's essence (the next point filling as it recharges) and a death knight's six runes (ready ones on the left, the others filling as they come back); a Brewmaster gets a thin stagger bar under the frame instead, amber past 30% of your health and vermilion past 60%. A Devourer Demon Hunter gets a thin soul fragments bar under the frame (the Dark Heart stacks, or Silence the Whispers in Void Metamorphosis, against the game's cap), and an Augmentation Evoker a thin Ebon Might bar (the seconds left, against twenty; empty while the client hides its time) under it, besides the essence pips. Each follows a spec change (after a fight), and a value the client hides still fills the pips, without the parts. They come and go with the Class bars module.

## Combat timers

A swing timer with a row per hand (main hand, off hand, and ranged for a hunter's Auto Shot), from WoW Forever's own swing events. It sits just over your cast bar and rides up with it when the action bars' stack grows, so extra bars never cover it (a spot you dragged it to stays). It is on for warriors, rogues, hunters, paladins, shamans and druids (a row shows only while a swing runs), and can be set to always or never. A row dims to 40% while your target is out of that hand's reach, as the game's own timer does (when the client can't tell, it stays at full strength). While it is on, the game's own swing timer (its Show Swing Timer setting) is hidden; while it is off, the game's bars are drawn in the theme, each hand in its row's color. On your power bar (WoW Forever only), a spark counts down the five-second rule after each mana spell, or crosses the bar with every two-second energy tick (it goes after three sweeps with no tick, as at full energy, and starts again with the next one).

## Readouts

On WoW Forever only: your threat on your target as a percent above the target frame (in the threat colors once you have aggro), shown only in a group or with a pet (solo, every mob is on you at 100%), and a glyph on the pet frame for a hunter pet's happiness: a green check happy, an amber triangle content, a red cross unhappy, with the pet's health text ending just left of it; the pet frame's tooltip adds the mood and the damage it gives ("Happy: 125% damage"). Each shows only where the client answers. A **status chip** (off until you tick it under Unit frames, then reload) sits just left of the player frame, level with it (where the pet frame, its cast bar and the combo points are never covered by a long "Missing: ..." line), or, where there is no room left of the frame (the Like the game's layout puts it 20 from the edge, and a narrow screen has none), just above the frame's left end; always kept on the screen: the buff's own icon and "Eating", "Drinking" or "Well Fed" while that buff is on you, else, if Instance prep's buff checks are on, a warning glyph and "Missing: Flask +2" (the first thing those checks find missing and how many more) in the warning color. It reads your auras out of combat only: it goes the moment a fight starts, stays away through it and while the client hides aura values or you are dead, and shows nothing on a client without the aura lookup.

- Target and focus auras: debuffs under the frame (a crowd-control slot, then yours soonest-first, then others' dimmed if you turn "Only your debuffs" off; including the debuffs the game flags for nameplates, as its own target frame does), the target's buffs above it, each row wrapping at the frame's width. Timer numbers are off by default (the dark sweep shows time left) and can be turned on. On WoW Retail the target frame also has a spot just right of it, standing on top of the target of target (left of the target is the Cooldown Manager's row) where the game draws the target's first private aura (one a boss fight hides from addons), handed again on each new target.
- Cast bars for you, your target and your focus, with a spark, the seconds left, your latency at the end of your own cast, and a gray edge on a cast you can't interrupt (where the client hides whether a cast can be interrupted, the bar still takes the gray, without the edge); a long spell name ends in "…" before the seconds. On retail an empowered cast (an Evoker's) fills like a cast, to the end of its hold at the last stage, with a thin light mark where each stage ends (none while the client hides the stage times); one you let go of flashes done, one cut short reads Interrupted. Yours sits just above the action bars at the bottom of the screen and moves up when they grow (more bars on, or more rows), so they never cover it; a spot you dragged it to stays put, and all the cast bars draw over the action bars. Your pet's casts (an Imp's Firebolt, say) show on a small bar just left of the pet frame, movable in unlock mode, while your own cast bar is on and the game's own cast bars are put away (both the default), since the game's pet cast bar goes with them; the **Your cast bar (and your pet's)** switch turns both off. The game's own cast bar over the professions window while you craft stays, in the theme's bar and font (red, not green, when a craft is interrupted or fails). **Cast ring at the cursor** (Unit frames → Cast bars; off by default, applied at once) puts two thin rings on the mouse pointer: the outer one sweeps the global cooldown away, the inner one fills as your cast goes (and empties for a channel). They are drawn from the client's own duration objects, so they work in combat when the numbers are hidden; the ring takes no mouse input, so clicks and tooltips go through it, and it follows the pointer only while it shows. Interrupt mark on cast bars** (Unit frames → Cast bars; on by default, applied at once) puts a small diamond to the right of those two bars while their cast can be interrupted and you know an interrupt (Kick, Pummel or Shield Bash, Counterspell, Earth Shock (Wind Shear on retail), Silence; on retail also Mind Freeze, Disrupt, Skull Bash and Solar Beam, Counter Shot and Muzzle, Spear Hand Strike, Rebuke, Quell): a filled accent diamond when it is ready, a hollow dim one, darkened like a cooldown sweep, while it recovers. The sweep takes the client's own cooldown object, so it works in combat when the numbers are hidden, and the mark is not drawn for a cast you can't interrupt, for a class without one, or when the client won't say.

## Your buffs and debuffs

Your buffs and debuffs replace the game's buff frame (and the external defensives and deadly debuff frames of newer clients, WoW Forever's among them): buffs in rows left of the minimap with timers (12 to a row; on a screen too narrow for that beside the main tanks' spot, under 1254 UI units wide, fewer to a row, never under five, so the block grows down instead; a block you moved keeps its row), your debuffs on a new row under them (edged in their type's color); right-click a buff to cancel it. On every aura icon the timer (when timers are on) sits on the icon's bottom edge, the stack count in its top right corner, and a typed debuff's small type letter in the top left corner, so none of the three covers another on a 30 icon. Weapon enchants (poisons, stones, oils, and a shaman's imbues on WoW Forever, which the game's own enchant cells miss; on retail too, read through its own enchant call) sit in front of the buffs. Logging in or reloading during a fight leaves the game's buff frames up until the fight ends, then Vanguard's buffs replace them. Move them with `/vg unlock`. Their settings (timer numbers, weapon enchants, icon size) are on the Unit frames page, after the target and focus auras; the Map page, where they used to be, points there. On WoW Retail a row of six spots stands just left of the block and its weapon enchant cells (outside it, so the Calm HUD never fades it) where the game draws your own private auras (the ones a boss fight hides from addons), in place of the game's debuff frame that Vanguard puts away.

## Nameplates

Class/reaction/threat colors, the name in outlined text with the level (colored by how hard the unit is for you), your debuffs (soonest to expire first, with timers) and your crowd control (both including the auras the game flags for nameplates only, as its own plates do), casts, raid icon, an accent border, a 2-pixel accent glow and a diamond on your target (your focus, when it isn't your target, gets the border and diamond in the quieter second text colour, without the glow; both under the same switch) (every plate's one-pixel border and the glow, and the one-pixel edges of its debuff and buff icons, are painted again within half a second when the game rescales a plate, so a plate the game hands over tiny never keeps a thick dark border or dark squares around its icons; an icon the game has closed to addons, as on a plate it keeps for itself, is left as it is, by that check and by a theme change (accent, look, font, UI scale or window size) alike, and one that fails to place is reported once and not tried again), and a gold quest "!" (Vanguard's own mark) by the name of units your quests need: when the game says so plainly, or when the unit's tooltip data from the client lists a quest title or objective of yours (a hidden answer counts as no; a switch turns it off). Quest changes repaint the marks once a frame. Vanguard dresses every plate the game makes, except a unit the game shows only a widget for (some friendly NPCs), which keeps the game's plate, and never hides enemies, but the game makes none for hostile units while its Enemy nameplates setting is off, so when it is off Vanguard asks once per character (at a login after setup, with no other question open) whether to turn it on, and changes it only on a yes; after that it is yours. The game's soft-target icon (its Soft target icon options: the sword over a soft enemy, the speech bubble over someone you can talk to) shows above Vanguard's plate, over the debuffs. Widgets the game puts on other plates (progress bars on some enemies) are not shown. The Nameplates page in `/vg` has the game's Enemy nameplates (the V key), Friendly players' and Friendly NPCs' nameplates switches, and the game's Health percent and Health value boxes (its Nameplates → Info display), all applied out of combat, and `/vg plates` says how many plates were drawn and what the game's plate settings are. Players' plates show their buffs right of the bar: big and external defensives and important buffs (immunities, major cooldowns) on friend and foe, and the buffs you cast on friendly players (3 a row by default; none, 3 or 5), nameplate-only buffs included. A colour change (a look, Safer colours) repaints every plate's bar at once. A click lands on the plate as Vanguard draws it, the name and bar plus a little around them, and the client stacks plates by that same box; friendly players' plates stay clickable even with the game's name-only option for them on. The plate under the mouse gets a faint wash, so you can see which one a click will hit. Vanguard builds 40 plate frames ahead, two a frame after login rather than all at once (a fight pauses that), so a pull that brings many plates builds none. A plate first drawn during a fight uses Vanguard's own icons (the client's aura containers can't be made in combat) and switches to the client's when the fight ends. With the game's personal resource display on (its Combat options, or `nameplateShowSelf`), the game's own health and power bars under your character take the plates' look: the theme's bar texture on a flat panel, their numbers in the plates' outlined font, with the game's colours, values and place. While the game's Info display asks for the health percent or value (in its own options or on Vanguard's page; Vanguard never changes it on its own), enemy plates show them small and outlined at the bar's right end, the value left of the percent; friends' plates and plates at the smallest height show none. The same mark sits left of the level: a green check on mobs you have tagged, a grey X on mobs another player has tagged, whose bar also turns grey (over the threat colour). Switched off under `/vg` → Nameplates ("Tag marks").

## Main tank frames

In a raid, the players marked main tank or main assist get small tiles at the top left (beside the raid tools), with health in their class color (a dark bar while offline) and what each is targeting written beside it. Click one to target them; your click casting and hover keys work on them as on the raid tiles. Move them with `/vg unlock`; turn them off under Modules.

## Party pets and targets

Party pets and targets (off until you turn it on under Unit frames): a small tile for each party member's pet and target, one row per member, above the party frames (the Healing layout draws the party larger, 115%, so its grid reaches about 32 units into the column; move either with `/vg unlock`) (on a screen under 676 UI units tall, the UI scale slider's last notches, right of the party column instead, so it stays on the screen). They show only in a party, each while its unit exists. Click one to target its unit; the pet tiles also take your click casting and hover keys (the target tiles don't, so a click always targets an enemy).

## Group threat

Group threat (off until you turn it on under Unit frames, then reload): a small movable panel (`/vg unlock`; it starts at the top left, under the boss column, on a screen 1365 UI units wide or more, and at the right, left of the minimap and under your buffs, on a narrower one, where a 25 or 40 player raid grid reaches the left corner) listing up to five members of your party or raid by their threat on your target, highest first, as "Name  87%" (in the threat colors once a member has aggro). It shows only in a group with a hostile target, and updates in combat since nothing in it is protected. When the game hides threat values (`C_Secrets.ShouldUnitThreatValuesBeSecret`, or any value comes back hidden) the panel hides itself and never compares or adds up a hidden value. Whether Forever hides threat in combat is not yet tested in game (issue #223), so the panel may only show out of combat there.
