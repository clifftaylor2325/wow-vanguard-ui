# Quests

What Vanguard does here, in full. This page is part of the behaviour spec; the [README](../../README.md) has the overview and [docs/README.md](../README.md) lists every page.

## Quest dialog

Talking to a quest giver opens Vanguard's own dialog just above the frames around your character (clear of the cast bars) instead of the game's quest and gossip windows, sized to what the NPC has to say: the NPC's portrait (a quest mark in your accent color when there is none to draw, such as for a quest started from an item) and name in the window-title style (the dialog has no close button: Goodbye, alone at the far left of the buttons, and Escape end the talk), the text (a long text, or a long list of quests and options, scrolls with the mouse wheel or a thin scroll thumb with soft edges once the dialog reaches its greatest height, which is less on a short screen, where the dialog stops short of the screen's top), numbered lines for quests (in their difficulty colors; gold when ready to turn in) and gossip options, each quest line led by Vanguard's own mark (a gold ! for a quest you can take, a gold ? for one ready to turn in, a muted ? for one in progress), reward cards with quality borders lined up under the text, in rows under the game's own captions (the choices first, under "Choose one of these rewards:" on the reward page and "You will be able to choose one of these rewards:" before; a single choice and the rewards with no choice under "You will receive:"; what you get as well under "You will also receive:"; the items a hand-in needs under "Required items:"), a small gold crown in the top-right corner of the one choice that sells to a vendor for most (none on a tie, while a price is still unknown, or when nothing sells; the same pick as the automation switch below), and the money and XP you'll get. A quest that suggests a group says so in muted type under its objectives, in the game's words ("Suggested Players [3]"). A quest that costs money to hand in says so on its hand-in page ("Costs 5g 0s 0c", on the buttons' row just after Goodbye; the money and XP line, like it, ends short of the buttons at the right), and Complete asks first, naming the cost, as the game's own window does (No declines the hand-in and keeps your gold). Press a number to pick a line or a reward, Space or Enter for the main button (Accept, Continue, Complete), Escape to say goodbye (hiding the whole UI with Alt-Z hides the dialog with it and keeps the conversation open, so it is there when the UI comes back). In a fight only Escape works: the dialog lets the other keys go to the game and takes them back when the fight ends. An NPC with nothing but one option the game goes to directly (a flight master, say) opens it straight away, as the game's own window does, without the dialog. Movable with `/vg unlock`; switch it off under `/vg` → Quests to get the game's windows back.

## Quest tracker

Vanguard's own, in the tracker's spot under the info strip: each quest you watch as "[12] Title" in its difficulty color (a title too long for its line ends in "..."; the tooltip has it in full), its objectives as rows like the quest log's (each objective's name at the left and its count, such as 3/8, at the right edge; once done it turns muted gray with a check before it) or "Ready to turn in" ("Failed" in red in their place for a quest the game marks failed, a timed one run out, say), and its usable quest item as a button beside it (with its cooldown, a red tint while the target is out of range, and the game's item tooltip on hover). In combat the counts and text keep updating; the item buttons can't move then, so they take their new places when combat ends. Each quest also shows the XP it gives under its objectives, with thousands set apart ("1,250 XP"; `/vg` → Quests turns that off). A line at the foot of the tracker, "Quests: 62% of the XP to level", adds up the XP of every quest in your log (not only the watched ones) against the XP this level still needs; it is hidden at the level cap, when no quest in the log gives XP and when the client hides a number (a switch under Quests turns it off; on by default). A quest you accept is watched at once (a switch under Quests; on by default, and harmless where the game does that itself; the game still refuses past its watch limit). Left-click a quest to open it, Shift-click to point the arrow at it (Quest guidance), right-click to stop watching it; hovering a quest, challenge or recipe lights it with the list rows' hover wash and shows a tooltip with its title, its lines in full and a muted line naming its clicks ("Click: open · Shift-click: point the arrow · Right-click: stop watching"; no tooltip in a fight, and headings take no mouse); fold it with the chevron on its QUESTS header, which also shows how many quests you watch; order it as the game does or nearest first (`/vg` → Quests). Quests that don't fit are counted ("+3 more"); click the count to show more (the tracker grows down, up to 10 quests, and stops short of the screen's bottom so "Show fewer" stays in view) and "Show fewer" to fold them again; your profile keeps that choice after a reload or logout. The followed quest (the one you Shift-clicked or the game tracks, else the arrow's pick while the arrow shows) has a thin line in your accent down its left edge. A timed quest shows "Time left 4:32" under its title, counting down once a second (the game's own timer frame is hidden with its tracker; a timer the client hides shows nothing). Legacy challenges you track in the game (Forever's account-wide challenges) sit under a "Legacy challenges" heading after the quests, each with its criteria or "Complete"; right-click one to stop tracking it, as the game's own tracker does, and the heading shows only while one is tracked. Recipes you track in a profession window sit under a "Recipes" heading in the same way, each with its reagents as rows ("Linen Cloth" with "2/4" at the right edge, gray until you have enough, then muted gray with a check; a reagent the client hasn't named yet joins when it is); left-click a recipe to open it in the profession window, right-click to stop tracking it. The game itself watches at most 20 quests at a time. It sits under the game's windows, as the game's tracker does, so an open window such as the bags is never covered by it. The game's tracker is hidden while it shows; switch it off to get the game's back (in Vanguard's look). With Questie loaded, Questie's own tracker shows instead and Vanguard's stays out of the way, so there are never two; With Questie loaded under Quests picks Vanguard's instead (reload).

## Quest log

The game's quest log key and the quest log button on the bag and menu bar (and `/vg quests`) open Vanguard's own (an opaque window, kept on the screen so a narrow canvas never hides its quest list, with the faction crest faint behind its top-right corner) instead of the game's: your quests under their zones (each zone a small-capitals header with a chevron: click it to fold the zone; a long list, and a long quest text, scroll with the mouse wheel or a thin scroll thumb with soft edges), "[12] Title" in its difficulty color with a check when ready to turn in and the XP it gives in muted type at the right ("1,250 XP"; a switch under Quests turns that off; on by default), the chosen quest marked by an accent bar and a faint wash; the chosen quest's text, its objectives as rows under an "Objectives" header (a check and muted text once done; otherwise the text, its count such as 3/8 at the right edge and a thin progress bar under it), a line under them with the money the quest needs ("Required Money: 5g 0s 0c", in the danger colour while you carry less) and the group it suggests, and "Failed" in the danger colour in place of the objectives for a quest that failed (a timed or escort quest) and reward cards under the same captions as the dialog's, with the same crown (hover a card for the item's tooltip, here and in the quest dialog), and Track/Untrack (Shift-click a quest in the list does the same; when the game refuses, at its watch limit say, chat says so), Share (in a group) and Map along the right, and Abandon (through the game's own confirmation, which names the items the game destroys with the quest when it holds any; a danger button) alone at the far left. Ctrl-click quests to mark several (they look chosen, and the button reads `Abandon 3`): it then asks once, naming them and the items each destroys, and abandons them all. Clicking a quest in the tracker opens it here. With no quests the list reads "No quests yet. Quest givers show a ! over their heads." in muted type. Switch it off under `/vg` → Quests to keep the game's.

## Quest history

Quest history (on by default; `/vg` → Quests → "Remember which character finished each quest"): from the client's own `QUEST_TURNED_IN` only, Vanguard remembers for the whole account which of your characters last turned in each quest and when (one entry per quest, up to 1000 quests, the oldest dropped first). A quest you meet again says so in a muted line, "Done on Thrall-Forever · 3 days ago" (days by your computer's clock, as for auction prices), under the text and objectives in Vanguard's quest dialog and under the objectives in Vanguard's quest log ("Done on this character" for one you did here, a repeatable quest, say). With no turn-in remembered, a quest the game itself flags as done on this character reads "Done on this character" (so quests finished before Vanguard count too; the game rarely offers a quest it has marked done, so this shows mostly for a quest given again, and other characters' are known only from Vanguard's own record). The Quest history card's "List latest turn-ins" button (greyed while quest history is off) and `/vg quests history [n]` list the latest turn-ins (10 by default, at least 1 and at most 25) in chat, newest first, with who did each and when. Switched off, it records and shows nothing and keeps what it has.

## Quest guidance

Quest guidance (each part switched under `/vg` → Quests → Finding your way, the card it shares with the quest arrow and the minimap's quest spots), from the client's own data only, never a community quest database: a **New** mark in your accent after the title of quests new in WoW Forever, in the quest dialog, the quest log and the tracker, when the client flags them (Vanguard looks for a quest classification or tag named for Forever or New at login; without one nothing is marked, and `/vg questdata` prints what the client says about quests so the flag can be found); **quest-start pins** on the world map, a small gold "!" where a quest starts on the map shown, from the client's quest lines and the quest spots it marks as starts (hover one for the quest, its level in its difficulty colour and "Repeatable" when it repeats; click it to point the arrow there; Vanguard draws none on a map where the game's own map already shows quest offers, or while Questie or OpenMap is loaded); and the **arrow**: Shift-click a quest in the tracker and the game's own quest marker follows it (or, with **Shift-click points** set to a map waypoint, a waypoint on its next step: its next waypoint, else its spot on the map you're on; each falls back to the other), chat says which quest, and the tracker shows that quest's next step ("> Take the boat to Menethil") in the accent above its objectives when the client gives one.

## Quest arrow

Quest arrow (on by default; `/vg` → Quests → Finding your way): a small panel at the top centre of the screen with an arrow in your accent that turns toward the nearest unfinished objective among the quests you watch, the quest's title and the distance in yards. Within 15 yards it reads "Here" in green with a faint arrow (not while on a flight); a quest ready to turn in reads "Turn in · 230 yd" with the arrow in gold. A quest ready to turn in counts as nearer than it is, so its turn-in wins over an objective close by; the arrow keeps its quest until another is clearly nearer, and when it moves on from a finished quest chat says `Arrow: <title>.` (not during a fight). Shift-click a quest in the tracker to hold the arrow on it until it leaves your log; while it is held, a Pin button at the panel's right edge (the only part of the panel that takes the mouse) lets it go: chat says `Arrow: nearest quest.`, and the game's own tracked quest is let go with it, while a map waypoint (your `/way`, say) stays. With **Follow the nearest quest** off, the arrow follows only that quest or the game's own tracked one, and the Pin button hides the arrow until the next Shift-click (its tooltip says so). The spot is the quest's next waypoint, else its spot on the map you're on, from the client only; for a quest on another map the arrow points at the client's next waypoint on the map you're on (the zone exit or a boat), when the client gives one, else it reads `yd, no direction` with a faint arrow, and a quest with no spot says `No spot from the game`. Move it with `/vg unlock`.

## Quest spots on the minimap

Quest spots on the minimap (on by default; `/vg` → Quests → Finding your way): a small dot in your accent on the minimap where each quest in your log has its spot on the map you're on, and a gold `?` once it's ready to turn in, drawn over the dots; the quest the arrow follows has its mark a size larger. A spot farther than the minimap shows sits faint on its edge, in its direction (just inside the rim of a round minimap, never outside the map), and the marks turn with the minimap when it rotates. The spots are the client's own quest spots (one per quest); without them, or without the client's minimap scale, no marks are drawn.

## Quest automation

Quest automation (each off until you switch it on, under `/vg` → Quests; hold Shift while talking to skip it): accept quests, accept quests a party member shares with you (its own switch; a quest offered by a player, not an NPC, follows only that switch and says "(shared by Name)" in chat), turn in finished ones (it never chooses among rewards for you, unless you also switch on "take the one that sells for most", which leaves the choice to you when two rewards sell for the same or none sells at all, and says in chat which it took and why, "took Crested Buckler: sells for most", and it skips quests that cost money, leaving them to you), and skip the chatter when an NPC has just one quest for you. Each says in chat what it did; "Accepted" waits until the game has taken the quest into your log, so a quest it refuses (a full log, say) is not reported as accepted.
