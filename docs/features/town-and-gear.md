# Town and gear

What Vanguard does here, in full. This page is part of the behaviour spec; the [README](../../README.md) has the overview and [docs/README.md](../README.md) lists every page.

## Craft queue

Craft queue (off until you turn it on under Town and gear → Craft queue; `/vg craft`): a list of crafts to make, kept for each character. In a profession window, `/vg craft add [n]` queues n of the recipe on display; `/vg craft` lists the queue and the reagents you are still short of; `/vg craft next` opens the first recipe with its count typed in the cost panel's Crafts box, and you press Create; `/vg craft stock` raises Keep stocked to what the queue needs and makes the Restock list (with the Auction helper on; off, chat says so), and you buy; a craft you finish (your own cast, out of combat) comes off the queue; `done` takes one off and `clear` empties it. It also notes each profession cooldown it sees (`/vg craft cd` lists every character's, with the time left) and ranks the recipes you know whose prices are all known (`/vg craft top`: the best five, the profit of one craft before the house's cut and per hour at the seconds a craft takes, set under Craft queue; a loss shows with a minus, such as -5s 0c). Nothing is bought, posted or crafted for you. Forever documents neither a recipe list nor a recipe cooldown call, so where the client lacks them Vanguard reads the recipes it has met (the one on display, those queued, those with a cooldown noted) and takes a cooldown from the recipe's own spell; the ranking then covers only those. Select a recipe with a cooldown in its profession window and type `/vg craft cd`. Where neither source answers, the part that needs them stays quiet.

## Mail tools

Mail tools (off until you turn it on under Town and gear → Mail tools; `/vg mail`): beside the mail window (right of its panel, on the same layer, opaque, and closing with the mailbox however it closes), a panel with a search box and a filter button (All, Money, Items, Sales) lists the inbox mails that hold every word you typed in the sender, subject or first item, eight rows at a time (the mouse wheel scrolls them; the heading counts them, "1-8 of 23", and says "more on the server" while the game holds back mail past what the inbox can show); each row says how long its mail has left ("5d", "7h"), in the warning colour in its last 3 days, and then "deleted in 2d" for a mail the game says is deleted rather than sent back when it runs out; the panel opens at the top; **Take** takes a mail's money and its first item, one click each (a pay-on-delivery mail shows what it asks in its row, has no Take button and is never taken from here). **Take all** takes the money and every item of every mail that matches (its filter and words applied, those past the eight rows too), the last mail first, one step every third of a second, reading each mail again before every step (so it follows the mail as it empties, and waits while the game is still answering the last take), and **Return all** asks once, then sends the matching mails that can go back to their senders (a sale, a system or game master mail, or an empty letter stays); pay-on-delivery mail is never taken, Take all passes a mail whose sender or subject changed or that holds more than was listed, Return all one that is no longer the one listed (sender, subject, money, items, amount due and first item must all match), and both stop when you close the mailbox or a fight starts, then say how many steps went through of those planned (`/vg mail takeall`, `/vg mail returnall`). `/vg mail <words>` prints the same matches in chat. Presets send items to your alts: the panel's **+ Add** button (after the preset buttons, while there is room for one) asks for a name, the character and the words an item's name must hold (none means every item); pointing at a preset's button says what it sends to whom, and a right-click on it asks, then removes it. `/vg mail preset add <name> <character> [words]`, `/vg mail preset` (lists them) and `/vg mail preset remove <name>` do the same by typing. With the Send Mail tab open, a preset's button on the panel (or `/vg mail send <name>`) types the character in and attaches the matching items from your bags that can be mailed (not soulbound, not already attached), up to the game's 12, out of combat; you press Send. The panel says "Your inbox is empty." or "No mail matches." when there are no rows, shows only the buttons of presets you have made, and says "No presets yet." beside + Add while there are none. Nothing is taken, attached or sent without your click.

## Pet stable

`/vg pets` lists your pets, the one out first, with level, family, loyalty, happiness and how far each is to the next level, read from the game's stable (Forever's `C_StableInfo`); opening a stable master's window says in chat how many pets you have and what the next slot costs (under Town and gear → Pet stable: the switch, and a List my pets in chat button that does what `/vg pets` does). Where the client lists no pets it says so and stays quiet. Still to be checked in the game.

## Character sheet extras

Character sheet extras (`/vg gear`; Game windows → Character sheet extras): each gear slot of the character pane shows its item level along the top edge (durability stays along the bottom), and a switch (off until you turn it on) marks a worn piece of uncommon quality or better with a `!` when its slot can take an enchant (chest, legs, feet, wrist, hands, back, weapons; an off hand if it is a weapon or a shield, not a held item) and it has none. `/vg gear` (or the "Show my gear summary in chat" button) prints your average item level, your melee damage per second (the character sheet's figure) and swing time from the game's own figures, and the pieces missing an enchant. Values the client hides are left out.

## Other characters' items

Vanguard remembers what each of your characters on a realm and side (and ruleset, where the game names one) carries and keeps in the bank, and an item's tooltip lists who else has it under the count of your own: one line per character (the name in their class color, "5 (bank 3)"), at most six, then "+N more" and the total. Bags are read as they change; the bank only while it's open, so each character's bank is as it was on their last visit. `/vg inventory` lists the characters remembered and when their bags were read; `/vg inventory forget <name>` drops one and `/vg inventory clear` forgets them all. Each character's record also keeps its level, money and rested XP (a percent of a level), refreshed as its bags change and at logout, and the time played once you have typed `/played`: `/vg alts` lists every character's level, money, rested XP and time played with all their money together, and `/vg alts <name>` lists that character's bags and bank, the biggest stacks first (a chat list), and the same overview sits at the foot of the Game windows page under "Your characters". Characters remembered by an older version move once to the realm, ruleset and side you next log in with. On by default (Game windows → Tooltips). While the Forever beta loses saved settings between sessions, other characters won't be remembered from one session to the next.

## Gear upgrades

An item's tooltip says how it compares with what you wear ("Upgrade +12% (estimate) vs Ironwood Bracers", or a downgrade; "estimate" because the default weights below are not simulated values), an arrow marks upgrades in the combined bag, and another marks quest rewards that are upgrades. Items are scored by stat weights for your class and role (your group role, damage when you have none, or the role you pick under Town and gear → Gear upgrades): rings and trinkets compare with the weaker of the two you wear, a two-hander with both hands, and an empty slot is always an upgrade; gear you can't wear is left alone. The auction house's Upgrades list (under Auction helper) judges listings by the same weights. The default weights are a starting point for Forever's vanilla-style stats, not simulated values: `/vg weights wizard` (or Set up stat weights under Town and gear → Gear upgrades) opens a slider and an exact-value box for every stat, for the weights in use (the ones your class and role value first; Defaults puts them back, Save keeps them, Cancel changes nothing), `/vg weights` shows the ones in use, `/vg weights set AGI 1.2` changes one (0 makes a stat worthless; the command and the box take only ordinary numbers, not "nan" or "inf"), `/vg weights reset` goes back to the defaults, and `export`/`import` share them as a string. Each profile keeps its own.

## Vendor memory

Tooltips say which merchant sells an item (and where) once you've visited one: "Sold by Thomas Miller (Elwynn Forest, Goldshire)", with "limited stock" when the merchant carries only a few, for up to three merchants per item, the one you saw last first. The Keep stocked list (`/vg stock`) says where to buy each item too. What merchants sell is remembered for every character on the account; `/vg vendors` says how many merchants are remembered and `/vg vendors clear` forgets them. Two switches under Game windows (Tooltips): the tooltip line, and remembering at all.

## At a merchant

Repairs with your own gold and sells your gray items (tried again a few times, since the server limits how fast items sell, and only while the merchant is still open and you are out of combat). The error line drops what the action bar already shows (ability not ready, not enough rage or energy, no target; its switch is under Game windows, Quiet the game, beside hiding the whole line); errors you can act on stay, shown by the game's own error line (its sounds and its one line per kind of error, which refreshes instead of stacking). That error line, and the zone and subzone names the game shows as you enter a place, are in the theme font; so are the game's scrolling combat text over your character (when you turn it on in the game's Combat options) and the floating damage numbers over your target, which keep the game's own font when Font is set to the game's. Each can be turned off. Item tooltips show what the stack sells for (unless the game already shows a price) and how many you have, bags and bank, with an optional item ID line. The lines other modules add to item tooltips (other characters' items, Sold by, auction prices, upgrades) show whether or not the themed Tooltips module is on; switching Tooltips off takes away only its own price, count and ID lines and its look.

## Spell ranks

Spell ranks (both switches start off, under Town and gear): **Bar notice** says in chat, once and out of combat, when a bar button holds an older rank of a spell you know a higher rank of; `/vg ranks` lists those buttons, by the Vanguard bar and button that shows each ("Bar 2 button 5", or "Action slot 14" for one no Vanguard button shows now, such as another page of bar 1), and what each would become (nothing changes), and `/vg ranks swap` puts the newest rank on them, out of combat only and never with something on your cursor. **Trainer memory** remembers, for your class and the whole account, what each trainer you open teaches and at what level, with the trainer's name and place; `/vg trainers next` lists what the next five levels bring and where to learn it, a level-up says the same, `/vg trainers` says how many services are remembered and `/vg trainers clear` forgets them. Both read only your spellbook and the trainers' windows: no rank or level table ships with Vanguard, and nothing is read or changed in combat.

## Errands

A **Train all** button on the trainer's window shows how many one click learns (the skills your gold covers, taken down the list), learns them in one click (every purchase happens inside the click, as the game wants), and says what it cost in chat; its tooltip says what the whole list costs and how many of them you can afford ("5 skill(s) for 4g 20s; you can afford 3 (1g 10s)."), and while it is greyed out, why ("Not enough gold for any: the cheapest is 50s."). **Keep stocked**: `/vg stock <shift-click an item> 20` keeps 20 in your bags (the list is each character's own, even on a shared profile, and a profile export carries none); a merchant who sells it tops you up, a stack at a time and in whole merchant stacks (ammo comes 200 at a time), while you can pay, and only for gold (an item priced in honor, badges or other items is never bought for you); each buy must show in your bags before the next, and a buy that does not arrive in full (bags full, a stock limit, short of gold) ends the run with a line saying so, and so does closing the merchant, so chat only reports what you received (`/vg stock` lists them, with where to buy each once Vendor memory has seen a merchant selling it; `0` removes one, `clear` empties the list). The same list opens in a window from **Edit list...** under Town and gear → Errands: one row per item with its icon, name, where to buy it (or "where to buy: not seen yet"), how many to carry and Remove; drag an item from your bags onto the slot under the rows to add it with one stack (20 while the game doesn't know its stack size); an amount saves as you type, and 0 or an empty box takes the item off when you leave the box; **Clear list** asks first. The window only edits the list, so it opens in a fight too, and `/vg stock` changes show in it while it is open. The **flight timer** saves each flight's time by route, and the next flight on that route shows a bar (movable) counting down to landing. The **boat timer** (off until you switch it on) does the same for boats and zeppelins: a ride is spotted when the game gives you no map position outside an instance, a flight and death, and the bar counts down from the time of the route you took last from that dock (the place you got on, named by its sub-zone); its bar starts on the flight bar's spot, since you never ride both at once. Buying, training and the boat timer's checks stop in combat. Each has a switch under Town and gear.

## Instance prep

A card at the door listing what you're short of. Stepping into a dungeon or raid, it lists what is low, each with a red mark and "have / want": ammo (hunters), soul shards (warlocks), your class's reagents for your level (runes and powder, candles, seeds and berries, symbols, ankhs, powders), food and drink, bandages, potions, durability under 50%, and anything on your Keep-stocked list below its count; with nothing short it stays away. Buff checks (off until you switch them on under Town and gear) add "missing" rows to the card when a ready check or `/vg prep` shows it: a flask, the Well Fed food buff, a weapon oil, stone or poison on your main hand (and your off hand when it holds a weapon), and the raid buffs Power Word: Fortitude, Mark of the Wild and Arcane Intellect (or their group versions); "World buffs among them" adds a row when you have none of the world buffs. They are read from your own auras, out of combat only, and while the game hides aura values no row is made; the buff names are from the classic game and are not yet checked against Forever, so a row also counts as met when any buff on you has one of a few key words in its name (Flask of, Well Fed, Fortitude, Mark of the Wild, Gift of the Wild, Arcane Intellect, Arcane Brilliance), which keeps a Forever variant or a camp buff that stands in for a class buff from showing as missing; `/vg prep buffs` lists your buffs with the check each counts for, to check the names in the game. A ready check (or `/vg prep`) always shows it, saying "Everything's packed." when nothing is short. When something on it is one item you can buy (your class's reagents, your Keep-stocked items), its **Buy list** button (shown only while the Auction helper is on) makes the Auction helper's Restock shopping list from it. With **Dungeon card** on under Town and gear (off until you switch it on), the card also shows the dungeon you stand at (its size, difficulty and level range, as the client gives them), your own quests for it (in a dungeon, the quests the client puts on its map; elsewhere, the dungeon and raid quests in your log; lowest level first, "done" when ready to turn in) the keys in your bags and, once you have been there before, when you last came ("today", "yesterday" or "N days ago", kept for each character; a way back in within the hour of the last one is the same visit, however long the run), and it shows at the door even with nothing short. It uses only your quest log and the client's data: no dungeon, quest or loot database ships, so what the client doesn't tell it is left out. It fades after 20 s or on its close button, never checks in combat (`/vg prep` waits for the fight to end), and moves with `/vg unlock`. Under Town and gear: switch the door and ready-check cards, Hide after and the durability warning; **Set wanted amounts** (or `/vg prep setup`) opens a short window to set how many food, bandages, potions, and ammo (hunters) or soul shards (warlocks) to carry, with **Use suggested** for your level; `/vg prep want food 30` sets one amount directly. Anywhere, when a supply (food, bandages, potions, ammo, shards, class reagents, Keep-stocked items) drops under its wanted count, Vanguard says so once in chat ("Running low: ..."); it speaks again only after the supply has been back at its wanted count, never in combat, and not for what was already low when you logged in (`/vg` → Modules → Instance prep turns it off).

## Talent planner

Plan where your talent points will go before you have them, on the game's own talents window. Vanguard adds a bar along the window's foot: a **Plan** switch, **Spend planned** and a summary (points planned, bought and still to buy, the next pick, how many points wait, and how many ranks are unreachable (the tooltip says why); it ends before the game's Apply Changes button, on two lines at most, and hovering it shows all of it). With Plan on, clicking a talent sets the rank you want instead of buying it (left-click one more, right-click one back; the tooltip shows the talent and its plan); the planned rank sits on the talent as a badge in your accent, the next point to buy wears an accent ring, and a rank the plan can never reach (a talent it hangs from, or its row's points, missing from the plan) is marked red. Spend planned (or `/vg talents spend`) buys the next picks in a legal order, highest rows first, and applies them; if the game refuses a rank, it stops there and chat names that pick. At a level-up chat names the next pick; "Spend planned points by itself" (under `/vg` → Talents, off until you tick it) buys and applies them without asking. Plans are kept per character and spec (Primary, Secondary); after a patch that moves the talents, the ones still there are kept and the rest dropped, with a line in chat. `/vg talents` prints the summary, `/vg talents plan` flips plan mode, `clear` empties the plan, `export` and `import` share it as a string (for your class only). Everything reads the game's trait API; a client without it gets the bar and nothing else.

## Legacy

Legacy (Forever's account-wide perk tree of 16 points and its challenges): `/vg legacy probe` lists, read only and out of combat, what the client has for the system (whether the Legacy window is loaded, the keys of its tree, challenge and reward pages, the perk panel's ids and node count, and the globals with Legacy in their name), so a challenge tracker can be built on what is really there; open the Legacy window once first. The **Legacy perk planner** (off until you switch it on under Town and gear) keeps a list of the perks you mean to take for the whole account, typed by hand and never over 16 points in all: `/vg legacy plan add <points> <name>` (the same name again changes its points), `remove <name>`, `clear`, and bare `/vg legacy plan` lists them with the points left. Nothing in the game's tree is changed, and no perk or challenge names are built in.
