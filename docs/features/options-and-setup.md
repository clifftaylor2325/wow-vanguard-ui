# Options and setup

What Vanguard does here, in full. This page is part of the behaviour spec; the [README](../../README.md) has the overview and [docs/README.md](../README.md) lists every page.

## Situations

Record a different layout and settings for raids, PvP, instances or resting; they apply out of combat as you move between them. One situation holds at a time, the first of PvP (a battleground or arena), Instance (a dungeon or raid instance), Raid, Party, Resting and Solo. To set one up, pick **Record for: Raid** on the Situations page (or `/vg situation record raid`), move frames with `/vg unlock` (spot, scale, opacity, links) and change settings as usual, then **Stop recording and save** (`/vg situation stop`; the button is greyed out while nothing is recording): only what you changed is kept. While you record, a banner along the top of the options window, and at the top of the screen while frames are unlocked, says "Editing as: Raid · changes apply only in Raid"; its **Stop and save** button ends the edit, saves what changed (chat says what was kept) and puts your own layout back. The banner also goes when the frames lock (by hand or for combat), on a profile change and at logout. An override can hold any frame's spot, scale and opacity, what it's anchored to, the sizes it matches and its own size, the info strip's, menu bar's and XP bars' visibility, the info strip's blocks, the nameplates' levels, threat colors, target border and quest marks, and the Calm HUD, and the Situations page lists them; nothing else is recorded. Any other setting, including every one that needs a reload, changes your own profile even while you record: while you record, the options dim most of those settings, and pointing at one says "Not recorded for Raid: this setting changes your base profile." Leaving the situation puts your own layout back, and your profile is always saved and exported with your own values. A change to something the active override sets lasts until you leave the situation. The page says which situation you're in and what its override changes; switch them all off there. Its **Your situations** card lists every situation with an override and what it changes, "(now)" on the one you're in, each with a **Clear** button (greyed while that situation is being recorded or the frames are unlocked); with none it says "No overrides yet. Pick Record for to make one." With the Situations module off under Modules, no override applies and the page says so. Profiles carry their overrides (an export string has them too, as overrides).

## Presets and options links for other addons

`/vg` → Profiles lists presets, profile strings you apply with one click (after a question, and a backup of your profile first): Vanguard's own Healer, Damage and Tank (raid frames, raid auras, raid healing and nameplates only; your layout and everything else stay; Healer and Damage are what setup's Healing and Damage and tanking layouts set) and any an addon offers with `Vanguard.RegisterPreset(name, string, info)`. `/vg preset <name>` does the same from chat or a macro, and `Vanguard.OpenOptions(page, setting)` lets an addon or macro open the options window on a page and row. A preset an addon offers after you opened the Profiles page shows there at once, and one it offers again with a changed string applies the new string. Nothing is applied until you say yes, and not in combat. Addon authors: see [docs/API.md](../API.md).

## Options

`/vg`, the addons button, or the **Vanguard** button at the top of the game menu (Escape), in an opaque window (the world doesn't show through it, under a short page either). Each page shows its name along the top, then a tab for each of its cards (Chat: Chat, Chat tabs, Chat extras and so on; the tabs run onto a second line when they don't fit one), and one card at a time under them: click a tab to show its card. A page opens on the tab it showed last (until you log out), a tab with a setting changed from its default wears the small accent dot, and a page of one card (Nameplates, Talents) has no tabs. What sits above a page's tabs stays whichever tab shows: the Modules page's note and Show choice, the Cooldowns page's setup button. A card scrolls with the mouse wheel or a thin thumb beside it when it is taller than the window. The window opens three quarters of the screen tall (at most the screen less 50, and never under 600), and fits again after a UI scale or screen size change. When Vanguard has caught errors this session, the addons button carries a small accent-coloured count (99+ at most) and the options window's footer says how many (the line stops short of the reload notice, ending in "…" when it runs long, and the notice shows only while a change waits for a reload; click that line to open them in the copy window, as `/vg errors` does; that window has **Report a bug** (as wide as its label needs), which opens the feedback form on a bug with the report attached, and **Clear**, which empties the list); with none, or after `/vg errors clear`, there is no badge. Pointing at the addons button shows Vanguard's version, what each click does (left-click: options; right-click: move frames, out of combat) and how many errors were caught this session. The game's AddOns list files Vanguard under a "UI Overhaul" heading where the client groups addons that way. Turn any module off under Modules to keep the default one. A setting changed from its default gets a small accent dot by its caption (point at it to see the default, such as "Default: On" or "Default: Follow the theme"; click it to put the default back), and a page holding any such setting gets one in the list on the left. A setting that applies only after a reload wears a small grey "reload" tag after its caption (point at the row for "Takes effect after /reload."); every module's switch on the Modules page wears "Reload to apply" the same way, since a module is built only at login. Typing in the search box lists the settings that match under it, each with the page it is on; click one, or press Enter for the first, to open its page on the tab that holds it, with the row marked ("colour blind", "colourblind", "color-blind" and the like find Safer colours, "font size" finds Text size, and "changed" lists every setting that differs from its default). The search also finds buttons ("move frames" finds Move frames; a button you pick is only marked, never pressed), the names in a choice ("square" finds Corners), the small note under a setting, and "reload" finds the settings that apply after a reload. Once a language is translated, the search matches a setting by its translated words or its English ones, so a guide written in English still finds it. It lists eight at most; past that its last line says how many more there are ("12 more: type more words"). A page with settings that have defaults has a **Reset this page** button at the right of its name, which asks, then puts every changed setting on that page back, on all of its tabs. The Modules page puts its switches on a tab for each of the names in the list on the left, with a **Show** choice (All, On, Off) above the tabs. A module that is on there while its own switch inside is still off (the meter, mail tools, chat history and the others that start off) says so in small muted text beside its name, such as "On, but its own switch is off (Combat tools)", until you switch it on on that page; the note stays on one line and ends in "…" short of the switch when a translation runs long, and pointing at the row reads it in full. The list has a page for each area: the unit, raid and action bar pages, Quests, Nameplates, Situations and the rest, then **Bars and bags** (XP and reputation bars, bags and bank, the menu strip and the quest tracker), **Map** (minimap, waypoints, your buffs, the info strip, other addons' feeds), **Chat** (the chat window, chat watch, the group board), **Game windows** (the game's windows, auto-accept, tooltips), **Cooldowns** (the game's Cooldown Manager, Vanguard's gaps row and the buff glow, with a setup window) and **Combat tools** (the game's damage meter and alerts, the meter, boss mods, fight history, the roll tracker) and **Town and gear** (the auction house, merchants and errands, the craft queue, mail tools, spell ranks, Legacy, instance prep, gear upgrades). These pages were one page, Bars, map, chat, before; with many pages the list's entries get a little shorter so they all fit, and a long page name is cut short before the changed-setting dot rather than running past the list. A module's row on the Modules page, and a switch with a long caption, grow to hold their wrapped text, at Larger text or in a longer language too; a page opened before the Text size changed is laid out again the next time you open it. With the window open, Up and Down go through the pages, Left and Right through a page's tabs (they step a slider instead while the keyboard is on one), and Tab (Shift-Tab back) through the settings the page shows, its Reset this page last, an accent ring marking the one Tab is on, Enter or Space pressing it; other keys still reach the game, and nothing is taken while you type in the search or a box, or in a fight. The top of General shows a unit frame (with your character's name) and an action button (with Vanguard's crest) in your accent, font, text size and bar texture as you change them. A card's buttons share one width, wide enough for the longest label in it (in any language), and a dropdown's list opens as wide as its longest choice, so no label runs past its button or under the list's check mark; a choice row's dropdown also widens, into the room its caption leaves spare, when its longest choice needs more than its usual width. Most changes apply at once; the rest show a Reload now button (as wide as its label needs, in any language and at any Text size). WoW Forever lets an addon reload only from a click on a secure button, so that's what Vanguard's Reload buttons are; one built during a fight becomes one when the fight ends, and until then it asks you to type `/reload`. The last page, **Feedback**, opens the feedback form on a bug or an idea and copies a plain report, and its **Community** card links Vanguard's Discord server and its X account (@WowVanguardUI): **Copy Discord invite** and **Copy X link** put the address in the copy box, selected, to paste into a browser, since an addon can't open a web page.

## Other addons

At login Vanguard notices addons that do a job one of its modules also does (bag addons such as Bagnon, AdiBags, ArkInventory, BetterBags, Baganator and Sorted; nameplate addons such as Plater and Platynator; EllesmereUI's unit frame, action bar, nameplate and raid frame parts; action bar (ElvUI and Tukui count here), unit frame, raid frame, click-casting, cast bar, minimap and tooltip addons; chat addons such as Prat, Chattynator and Glass; Auctionator and TradeSkillMaster for the Auction helper; window skinners such as Aurora and Forevermore Classic UI; window movers such as BlizzMove, MoveAny and Movable Windows Forever; and BetterFriendlist) and says so once in chat. The Modules page of `/vg` then shows an **Other addons** card with **Turn off** or **Keep both** for each, and the button names what it turns off: a module (it goes off, with a reload offered), or for the window addons only that part of Game windows (a window skinner: every window look group, since it restyles popups, menus and tabs too; a window mover: Vanguard's window dragging; BetterFriendlist: the social windows' look, which covers friends, the guild window, chat channels, the raid tab, letters and the social toasts; each with a reload offered, and the windows' durability and the other groups stay on). For Clique the card adds that Vanguard's unit and raid frames aren't signed up with Clique, so its bindings don't work on them (turn Vanguard's click casting off and those frames have none). Nothing changes until you click, and an answer isn't asked again in that profile. While that addon is still loaded the card keeps the answer with a button to change it: **Turn … back on** after Turn off (it puts back only what the card turned off), or **Turn off …** after Keep both.

## First-run setup

The first time you log in on an account, a four-step wizard asks for a layout (Damage, Tanking, Healing, Versus deck, Like the game's, or Keep my current layout, which moves nothing; the layout for your role, by specialization or else by group role, is marked "your role": Healing for a healer, Tanking for a tank, Damage for anyone else), sizes for your screen, an accent (color swatches), a raid frame style (three small raid tiles, each drawn in its style in your class color) the Calm HUD switch (off unless you turn it on) and two readability switches, **Larger text (115%)** (Text size 115%) and **Colour vision: Safer colours** (the General page's Safer colours switch; its colors settle at the reload Done offers), both off unless you turn them on, then unlocks your frames so you can move them (its page says that a click on a handle and the arrow keys nudge it, that a right-click gives a frame's anchor, scale and opacity, and that the bar at the top saves and locks, or exits without saving) (the same unlock mode as `/vg unlock`, or **Move frames** under General in `/vg`; `/vg lock` or Done locks them again). It opens on Keep my current layout and Keep my current sizes, the first time too, so Vanguard starts from where you are: Next, Next, Next, Done changes nothing. The window is as tall as its longest step needs, so every choice's description, the questions (which wrap) and the move step's lines stay above its buttons, translated or at Larger text too; a choice's name too long for its row is cut short. The screen step offers 1080p, 1440p, 4K and ultrawide presets and Pixel-perfect, and marks the one for your screen "your screen": each sets the game's UI scale (1080p 80%, 1440p and ultrawide 70%, 4K 65%, the bottom of the game's own range; it sizes the unit frames, which every layout draws in UI units), the action button size (38 px, the default), the raid tile size (100 x 48, or 110 x 48 on the bigger screens; it shows after a reload, which setup offers) and the text size (100% to 110%). **Pixel-perfect (sharpest)** instead leaves the game's UI scale alone and turns on Vanguard's pixel-perfect scale, so every line lands on whole screen pixels: one UI unit is one screen pixel, or two on a screen about 1600 pixels tall or more (4K), and it grows your screen preset's action buttons and text to make up for the smaller scale (raid tiles keep the preset's size). The UI scale is the same setting as the game's Options → Graphics → UI Scale, applied out of combat; the other presets turn Vanguard's pixel-perfect scale off, since that would replace it. The step says which sets your UI scale now (the game's slider, or Vanguard's pixel-perfect scale while it's on), and that a preset moves the game's slider. Before setup first changes anything in a run (a layout or screen preset applied, an accent or raid style picked), it copies the profile in use to its backup profile ("<name> backup", the same one imports use) and says so in chat. Done always ends with a reload offer (after the export question on a first run), so everything setup set shows as it will from now on. It waits until you're out of combat. Done finishes it, and so does **Skip setup** on its first step (which applies nothing; its button is as wide as the room left of Back, so a long translation of the label fits); the close button and Escape only close it, and it opens again at your next login until it's finished. Each new character on an account that has finished setup gets it too, with a Profile step first: share the profile your other characters use (the one used last, at the last logout of any of them; the character starts on it; changes there change them too), or give this character a profile of its own, named after it, that starts as a copy of that one, with no reload; Back doesn't return to the Profile step once a profile of its own is made, or once setup has changed anything in the shared one (so a change made there never stays in it after a later switch), and setup opened again (`/vg setup`, or the next login after closing it early) doesn't ask again. Characters that played before this came in aren't asked. To run it again: `/vg setup`, or **Run setup** under General → Layout in `/vg`; run again (or on a new character sharing a profile), it starts on Keep my current layout and Keep my current sizes, so clicking through changes only what you pick. **Start over** on the Profiles page asks first (saying every setting in the profile goes back to the defaults, for every character using it), copies the profile to its backup profile, resets it and opens setup after the reload. **Reset profile** asks and backs up the same way, and the profile list's **Delete** and **Copy here** say in a tooltip what they lose and ask "Sure?" on the first click (leaving the page or closing the window takes the question back, so a later single click only asks again). Reset profile, Start over and Copy here into the profile you use (or `/vg profile copy`) replace that profile's settings (Copy here keeps the profile's own craft queue and profession cooldowns, which are not settings) while its frames keep the old ones until the reload, so the options window's footer then says "Profile replaced: reload before you change more" instead of "Some changes need a reload". Damage is Vanguard's defaults (picking it puts back anything another layout moved or sized) with a taller target frame (40 instead of 36, growing up from its bottom edge) and the Damage preset's settings (no power bars, role sort, HoTs, incoming heals or buff watch on the raid frames, two raid debuffs, six debuffs with timers and threat colours on nameplates, and no buffs on players' nameplates); Tanking is the same with the tallest target frame (44) and the Tank preset's settings (role sort and the aggro strip on the raid frames, threat colours, the target border and three buffs on nameplates); Versus deck is your role's centered layout (with its settings) with the Versus deck turned on and your frame and your target's at one scale and height, and any other layout turns it off; Like the game's puts your player and target frames along the top left as the game has them (the pet under the player, the target of target and focus beside the target, clear of the target's cast bar and its interrupt mark), the party and raid down the left side, the boss frames on the right under your buffs, and the raid tools and main tanks near the top center; Healing turns click casting on, puts the party and raid frames back at their default spots (over your player frame, a raid of 25 or 40 stacked at the left of your character; see Centered layout) drawn larger (115%, for a group of up to 25 players: a raid of 26 to 40 keeps its compact size so the grid stays clear of the main tanks), draws your player and target frames smaller (90%) and sets the Healer preset's settings (power bars and role sort on the raid frames, three raid debuffs, HoTs, incoming heals, shields and the buff watch, two debuffs, no threat colours and no buffs on players' nameplates; the frames on screen change at once, the aura rows after the reload Done offers). Healing doesn't change how the groups are laid out; that's **Groups as** on the Raid frames page in `/vg` (Columns, the default, or Rows; a change takes a reload). A test checks all three layouts with groups as columns and as rows, solo and with 5, 25 and 40 players (raids above 10 are drawn smaller), on a 1365 x 768 screen at UI scale 1, on 1920 x 1080 pixel-perfect, with each screen preset on the screen it's made for, and with Pixel-perfect at 1080p, 1440p, 4K and ultrawide: with Vanguard's default spots nothing overlaps and nothing leaves the screen. To put the raid frames somewhere else, drag them with `/vg unlock` (or **Move frames** under General in `/vg`).

On a screen whose canvas is narrower than 1365 UI units at your UI scale now (a 16:10, 4:3 or 5:4 monitor at the game's own scale, the Steam Deck, or a 16:9 screen above 100%), the screen step also offers **Fit my screen: UI scale N%  ·  suggested**, just above Keep: it sets the game's UI scale to the one that makes the canvas 1365 units wide, the narrowest the layout is made for (90% on a 16:10 screen, 75% on 4:3, 70% on 5:4; never under the slider's 65%), and keeps every size as it is. A preset on such a screen sets a UI scale no higher than that one either (the 1080p preset on a 1280 x 1024 screen sets 70%, not 80%). Setup still opens on Keep my current sizes. Below 1365 units Vanguard's default spots adapt where they can (see Centered layout under Look and theme), and a test checks them on 1280 x 800, 1229 x 768, 1024 x 768 and 835 x 668.

## Language

Vanguard's own text (the options window, setup, chat lines and tooltips) shows in your client's language: German, French, Spanish (Spain and Latin America), Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Korean and Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), and English everywhere else. The translations are new and machine-drafted, so some wording will be off: report a wrong or clumsy line with `/vg feedback` or on the website's Feedback page, and any text without a translation shows in English. The command words you type stay English in every language, and so do the What's new notes and the diagnostics meant for a bug report (`/vg status`, `/vg report`, the `/vg errors` text, `/vg probe`, `/vg perf`, `/vg mem`, `/vg frame`, `/vg realm` and `/vg factionart`), so a report reads the same whoever sends it. Vanguard loads on every client language, and names the game supplies (spells, items, zones) stay in your client's language. On Korean and Chinese clients it uses the game's font, since its bundled font has no Hangul or Han, and on a Russian client the faction look keeps Fira Sans for headings, since the faction fonts have no Cyrillic. Where Vanguard looks for something of the game's by name (click casting's spell groups and starter layouts, the instance prep buff checks and class reagents, raid healing's class buffs, the status chip's eating, drinking and Well Fed), it lists the spell or item by id and takes the name from your client, so it works in every client language; trainer memory finds a spell's rank ("Rank 3", "Rang 3") by its number, not the English word.

## Settings guard

WoW Forever's beta has been reported to lose addon settings between sessions, so at each logout Vanguard leaves the same stamp in the account's settings file and in the character's own file, with a backup of the character's profile (as a profile string) beside the character's stamp. If at the next login the account file comes back blank, or older than the character's last session (each character is judged by its own stamp, so every character is asked after a loss, not only the first to log in), chat says the saved settings didn't load, a question offers to restore the profile from the character's backup (or, without one, to paste a profile string you exported), and the General and Profiles pages in `/vg` start with the same notice, with **Restore the backup**, **Paste a profile string** and **Start fresh: run setup**; setup waits meanwhile. A restore offers the reload that applies it. Until you answer (or import or pick a profile), logout leaves the stamps and the backup as they were, so the next login offers it again. Importing a whole profile string answers it and the notice goes, even a string that matches the profile you use. While the profile a backup names is missing (after a pasted string or a fresh start), logout keeps that backup rather than replacing it. If the character's own file comes back blank while the account file remembers the character, the character goes back to the profile it used (after a reload, which is offered) and setup isn't asked again. A first run, or files saved before this came in, have no stamps, so none of this fires. Finishing setup for the first time (Done, not Skip) asks whether to export a profile string to keep outside the game. Whether an addon can stop the game overwriting its settings at logout is unknown, so Vanguard only notices and restores. The game also refuses an addon's saved file past a size it doesn't publish, so about 15 seconds after login (out of combat) Vanguard estimates the size of the account file (the text the game writes, walked once); over a 16 MB budget (room for about four full price homes) it drops the auction prices of the realm-and-side homes with the oldest prices, never the one you play on, and says how many in chat. If the game sends its own too-large signal (`SAVED_VARIABLES_TOO_LARGE`, which means it could not load the file and will not save it this session) Vanguard drops nothing, since what it holds is not the file's, and says so in chat with what to do: log out and move `Vanguard.lua` out of the account's SavedVariables folder to start fresh; a file the game then returns blank is caught by the guard above. `/vg mem` first prints the estimate (account file against the budget, its three largest keys, and this character's file; one walk, out of combat).

## What's new

The first login after an update shows a short **What's new in Vanguard x.y** window: what changed in this version, grouped by module under small grey capitals headings, with "(off by default)" after a new thing that starts off. A line in the accent colour has a setting: click it and the options window opens on its page with that setting marked (the hint under the title, "Click a line to open its setting.", shows only when a line has one). It shows once per version for the whole account, out of combat, and never on top of setup: a new account goes through setup and skips it, and a new character sees it once its setup ends. `/vg whatsnew`, or **What's new** under General → Layout in `/vg`, opens it again; its lines are laid out again if the Text size changed since it was last shown.
