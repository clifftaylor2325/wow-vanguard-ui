# ForeverUI

A from-scratch UI suite for WoW Forever (the 12.1 engine with vanilla content; the TOC declares Interface 16001 for Forever and 120007).
Written from scratch. It contains no code, art or fonts from other UI addons. It bundles three small shared libraries, unmodified, in `Libs/`: LibStub, CallbackHandler-1.0 and LibSharedMedia-3.0, which addons use to share fonts and bar textures (see Credits).

## What's in it

- **Look and layout**: the Slate & Brass theme: flat slate panels (floating windows get a soft shadow; frames on the HUD stay flat unless you turn on Soft shadows under frames), a brass accent (or arcane, fel, void, ember), bundled Fira Sans Condensed, pixel-perfect borders, smooth bars (Forever, Soft or Flat bar texture), shared fonts and bar textures (the Font and Bar texture pickers under General also list every font and bar texture other addons share through LibSharedMedia, and ForeverUI shares its own font and bars the same way; if the addon behind your pick is gone, Fira Sans Condensed and the Forever bars come back), soft sparks on cast and power bars, quiet fades (never longer than 0.15 s in combat) and interface sounds (Reduce motion and Interface sounds switches turn them off; every fade and sound is listed in `docs/THEME.md`). Every frame can be moved in unlock mode (`/fui unlock`), with a grid, snapping and arrow-key nudging; right-click a frame's handle to set its anchor, exact offsets, scale (50% to 200%) and opacity (10% to 100%), or pick a frame under General → Layout and use its Scale and Opacity sliders. A scaled frame grows around its anchor point, which stays where it was; a frame that fades by itself (a mouseover bar, say) still fades, at its opacity. Scale and opacity are saved with the frame's position, go with the profile (export, import, switching), and `/fui reset` puts them back to 100%. The game's Edit Mode shows ForeverUI's handles too, with a note under its window, so you can move everything in one place (a switch under General turns that off). ForeverUI's frames replace the game's, so Edit Mode shows no handle for a game frame ForeverUI has hidden (the game's action bars, XP and reputation bars, unit, boss and cast bar frames, the quest tracker while ForeverUI's is on, and the rest); it keeps every system ForeverUI doesn't replace. Profiles can be exported and imported, and can switch with your spec (on clients that have specializations). A whole-profile string also carries the UI scale and screen size it was made on: importing one asks whether to use its UI scale (the game's own UI scale setting, applied out of combat), then offers the reload. A profile remembers which of the game's Edit Mode layouts you use with it (that goes in its string too): when a character loads a different profile than last time (a switch, a spec profile or an import), ForeverUI switches Edit Mode to that layout, out of combat, if your account has one of that name, and says so in chat; characters sharing a profile keep their own layouts. Choosing a profile (the list under `/fui` → Profiles, the picker at the top of the window, `/fui profile <name>`, an imported whole profile or a spec change) takes effect when you reload: until then the frames on screen still belong to the profile you were using, so moving a frame or changing the accent changes that one, and the Profiles page says which profile comes next (**Use** on the profile in use takes the switch back).
- **Centered layout**: everything near the action is anchored to the bottom center of the screen, so the layout keeps its shape at any UI scale. Player and target sit either side of your character with your cast bar and swing timer between them, the pet and target of target outside them, the focus above the player, and three bars of 38 px buttons under them over thin XP and reputation bars. Party and raid frames grow up from just above your player frame, centered on it (Setup's healing layout centers them above the middle of your frames), and a raid of more than 10 is drawn smaller (80%, or 72% above 25) so all 40 fit. Chat starts in a bottom-left spot under the frames, sized once; move it with `/fui unlock` (the game's own layout and your resizing are left alone after that), or switch the spot off under Bars, map, chat. A test checks that nothing overlaps or leaves the screen, solo to 40-man, at UI scale 1.0 and pixel-perfect, and with each of setup's screen presets on the screen it is made for. Frames you moved keep their spot; `/fui reset all` puts everything back.
- **Unit frames**: player, target, target of target, focus and party frames, and boss frames (up to five, in a column from the top-left corner, each shown while its boss is in the fight; the game's own boss frames are hidden). The player and target frames are 240 x 36 with 12 px names, sized for a 1080p screen at setup's 80% UI scale. On WoW Forever they show levels (colored by difficulty) and health as a percent, and a diamond marks elite, rare and boss targets. Combo points for rogues and druids above the player frame. Under it, shamans get their totems (icon and time left; right-click one to destroy it) and druids their mana while bear or cat form shows rage or energy: the game keeps both inside its own player frame, which ForeverUI replaces.
- **Combat timers**: a swing timer with a row per hand (main hand, off hand, and ranged for a hunter's Auto Shot), from WoW Forever's own swing events. It is on for warriors, rogues and hunters, and can be set to always or never. On your power bar, a spark counts down the five-second rule after each mana spell, or crosses the bar with every two-second energy tick.
- **Readouts**: your threat on your target as a percent above the target frame (in the threat colors once you have aggro), and a dot on the pet frame for a hunter pet's happiness: green happy, orange content, red unhappy. Each shows only where the client answers.
  - Target and focus auras: debuffs under the frame (a crowd-control slot, then yours soonest-first, then others' dimmed if you turn "Only your debuffs" off), the target's buffs above it, each row wrapping at the frame's width. Timer numbers are off by default (the dark sweep shows time left) and can be turned on.
  - Cast bars for you, your target and your focus, with a spark, the seconds left, your latency at the end of your own cast, and a gray edge on a cast you can't interrupt.
- **Your buffs and debuffs** replace the game's buff frame: buffs in rows left of the minimap with timers, your debuffs on a new row under them (edged in their type's color); right-click a buff to cancel it. Weapon enchants (poisons, stones, oils, and a shaman's imbues on WoW Forever, which the game's own enchant cells miss) sit in front of the buffs. Move them with `/fui unlock`.
- **Raid frames** (VuhDo-style):
  - Laid out in zones that never overlap: name at the top, status (missing health, Dead, Offline) under it, the missing-buff mark in the top-right corner, debuffs bottom left, your HoTs bottom right, the power bar along the bottom, and the ready check and resurrection big in the center. Default tile 100 x 48, drawn smaller in bigger raids. The missing-health text grows and shrinks with the tile but never drops under the theme's smallest text size on screen; on a grid drawn smaller (more than 10 players) it moves to the right end of the name row to keep that size.
  - Readable at a glance: class colors softened into the panel color by default, with a 1-pixel line in the full class color along each tile's top so close colors (shaman and mage blues, rogue and paladin yellows) still tell apart (or dark bars with class-colored names, or bright class colors), outlined names that end in "..." when too long, and a dark bar on the dead and offline. In instanced combat, where the game keeps health secret from addons, the bars still fill and the missing-health text hides.
  - Auras: the first debuff you can dispel, then up to two others (boss debuffs included); your HoTs, lit while they're in their refresh window; no timer numbers on these small icons. A debuff you can dispel washes the tile in its type's color from the bottom and colors its edge.
  - Healing: click casting and hover keybindings (a hover key can carry Shift, Ctrl or Alt: while the pointer is over a frame, Shift-Q casts on that player), incoming heals and shields, aggro strip, missing-buff marker (hidden while the game hides auras, so it never cries wolf), an accent edge on your target, a faint wash under the mouse.
  - Status: range fade, raid target icons, ready check (a check, cross or three dots in the theme colors), resurrection marker, and a gold crown for the leader and a small diamond for assistants. While the ready check or resurrection mark shows in the middle, the missing-health text steps aside.
  - Roles: a small shield for tanks and a cross for healers before the name; damage dealers get no mark. The raid's main tank gets the shield in your accent color, and the main assist in gray.
  - Groups as columns (the default) or rows: rows lay each group out side by side in one line, so a big healing raid is wide and short.
- **Click casting** (`/fui` → Click casting), set up without typing a spell:
  - **Starter layout**: one button fills the grid with your class's healing layout (Priest, Druid, Paladin, Shaman; Mage and Warlock get their buffs). Spells bind by name, so one you haven't learned yet starts working the day you learn it. Left click stays Target and middle click the unit menu.
  - **The grid**: mouse buttons down the side, Plain / Shift / Ctrl / Alt across the top (More modifiers adds the combinations); each cell shows the bound spell's icon. Click a cell to pick from your own spellbook, right-click to clear it, or drop a spell from the spellbook or a bar onto it.
  - **The picker** lists what you can cast on friends in groups (Heals, Protect & cleanse, Buffs, Other), with a search box and the spell's tooltip on hover. A spell with ranks binds as its highest rank unless you open its ranks and pick one (for downranking); then the cell says "Flash Heal (Rank 3)". Under the spells: Target, Unit menu, Focus, Assist, a macro, or a typed spell name.
  - **Hover keys**: Add key, press the key (Shift + Q, the wheel...), pick the spell; the row says when the key is also an action-bar binding ("also Action bar 1 slot 2"), which it keeps doing off the frames.
  - **Try it**: hover the sample tile and press or click; it says what would happen ("Shift + Right click -> Renew on you") without casting.
  - **Share**: Export and Import the bindings as a string, and copy them to another spec where the client has specs. Bindings are per character and spec; changes apply at once, or when the fight ends if you're in one.
  - **The game's click bindings** (where the client has its own click casting): a switch at the bottom of the page, off by default, uses the game's instead. ForeverUI's bindings and hover keys then rest (kept for when you switch back, and `/fui binds` says so), and the bindings you set in the game's Click Casting window (the page's button opens it) work on the raid and unit frames. The switch applies out of combat; turned on in a fight, it waits for the fight to end.
- **Raid tools**: for the group's leader and assistants, a small bar at the top left with a ready check, a 10-second pull timer (right-click cancels it), a role check and Raid (turns your party into a raid), the eight raid target icons (click to mark your target; the last button clears it) and the eight world markers (click to place or remove one; right-click clears it; the last button clears them all). It shows and hides out of combat.
- **Action bars**: range and power tint, plus restyled pet, stance and extra action buttons and a vehicle exit button. The restyle keeps what the game lights up by state: the active stance, the pet's attack flash and the auto-cast mark. The pet and stance bars sit in spots of their own at the bottom left, above the chat and clear of your bars (the game's own spot for them lies over the bar stack); move them with `/fui unlock`, or turn that off under Action bars to leave them to Edit Mode.
  - The bottom bars stack with bar 1 on top; turning on another bottom bar adds it underneath. Each row takes its bar's real height, so a bar with bigger buttons or more rows pushes the ones above it up, right away when you change it. A position you've dragged is kept; `/fui reset all` puts the bars back in this order.
  - Empty slots stay hidden until you drag a spell or item, so a new character sees no empty squares, and they take no mouse, so clicks and right-drag camera turns over blank bar space reach the game. A slot that fills or empties in combat (bar 1 paging into a form) shows or hides at once, but takes or releases the mouse only when combat ends; its key still works.
  - Hover a button for its action's tooltip, in the game's default spot; it follows the page bar 1 is on, and an empty slot shows none.
  - The game's self-cast, focus-cast and mouseover-cast options work on these buttons as on the game's own.
  - Keybind mode (`/fui keybind`, or **Keybind mode** under Action bars): hover a button and press a key (with Shift, Ctrl or Alt if you like) to bind it; the key replaces the button's keys, is saved in the game's own key bindings (for your account or this character, whichever you use), and shows on the button at once. A modifier pressed alone does nothing, a key pressed away from the buttons goes on to the game, and Escape leaves. Empty slots and faded bars show while it is on. It works out of combat only and ends when a fight starts.
  - Counts, icons and usability follow what you carry and wear (bags, equipment), your form, macro edits and spell charges, so a stack you use up or a form that changes an icon updates at once.
  - Each bar has its own number of buttons, buttons per row, button size, and visibility: Always, On mouseover (seen only while the mouse is over it), In combat, Hidden, or When checked below: a row of conditions under it (Combat, Mouseover, Group, Raid, Instance, Vehicle, Resting), and the bar shows while any checked one holds (with Mouseover checked it fades in under the mouse the rest of the time). The game itself shows and hides the bar on those conditions, in combat too; In an instance is re-checked whenever you change zone (after a fight, if you are in one). While you drag a spell or item, bars on mouseover or on conditions show too (the latter only when the drag starts out of combat).
  - Hidden bars keep their keys: the bar isn't drawn, but a bound key still casts, like a keybind-only bar. A Hidden bottom bar keeps its row in the stack (leaving a gap), because bars can't be re-stacked during combat.
  - The right-edge bars (4 and 5) start off; turned on, they show on mouseover. Profiles that already had them on keep them on Always.
  - With the Masque addon installed, Masque skins the buttons instead of ForeverUI: its options list four ForeverUI groups (Action bars; Pet, stance and extra buttons; Buffs and debuffs, with the weapon enchants; Target and focus auras), each with its own skin. ForeverUI bundles nothing of Masque; without it nothing changes.
- **Nameplates**: class/reaction/threat colors, the name in outlined text with the level (colored by how hard the unit is for you), your debuffs (soonest to expire first, with timers) and your crowd control, casts, raid icon, an accent border, a 2-pixel accent glow and a diamond on your target, and a gold "!" by the name of units your quests need (only when the game says so plainly; a switch turns it off). A plate first drawn during a fight uses ForeverUI's own icons (the client's aura containers can't be made in combat) and switches to the client's when the fight ends.
- **Text size**: one slider (/fui > General, 70% to 130%) sizes all of ForeverUI's text, chat and tooltips included, and applies at once. Chat has its own Small, Medium or Large on top (or keeps the game's own chat size if you prefer). `/fui status` shows your UI scale and how many pixels tall chat text draws on your screen.
- **The game's extras, tidied**: the game's own XP bar is hidden while ForeverUI's shows (no more double XP bar), and so are the durability figure, clock and calendar button while the info strip shows them (`/calendar` still opens the calendar). Tracking, mail, dungeon difficulty, the addon compartment and the group finder eye move onto the square minimap's corners, and the expansion button (nothing to show in vanilla content) is put away; the game's emptied minimap frame at the top right stops taking mouse clicks and its zone button is put away. The bag buttons and the micro menu share one bar in the bottom-right corner: the bags (the key ring too, where the client has one, on the same slot as a bag) in a row centered over the menu buttons, with a thin accent line between them under the slots. The bar hugs its slots with an 8-pixel margin all round and grows to the left from its bottom-right corner as the game adds buttons. Every button sits on a 24-pixel slot with a 4-pixel gap (a slate tile with a beveled edge, a brass inner edge in your accent and a small soft shadow) and keeps the game's own icon and alert flash; the brass edge brightens under the mouse and darkens while you press, and a disabled menu button turns grey. Each bag's slot has a thin bar along its bottom showing how full that bag is (red when it is full; a soul bag or quiver counts only its own slots), an empty bag slot shows the game's empty-slot art faint and grey, and the backpack shows how many slots your bags have free. ForeverUI's line icons can stand in for the game's menu icons (off until you switch them on; an earlier ForeverUI turned them on for everyone, so updating turns them off once). The bar is always shown; you can choose On mouseover, Auto-hide after 10 s (shown when you log in and while the mouse is over it, faded 10 seconds after the mouse leaves), In combat, Hidden or the same row of conditions as the action bars instead, under Bars, map, chat; it applies at once. The game's own bar frames are hidden inside it, and a spot you gave the menu strip is kept (a separate bag strip's spot is dropped, since the bags now ride in the bar). The quest tracker has its spot under the info strip: ForeverUI's own tracker fills it, and only with that switched off does the game's tracker sit there instead, with flat headers, a brass line under each, the theme's collapse arrows and the theme font (quest colors stay the game's). The bar sizes itself to its buttons, re-fits whenever the game lays the bars out again, and stays where it's put when the game lays out its own frames. Each has a switch under Bars, map, chat.
- **Game windows**: character, merchant, mail, quest and gossip (only when ForeverUI's quest dialog is switched off), trade, loot and roll windows, the game menu, the ready check, popups, the spellbook and the breath and fatigue bars get a flat ForeverUI panel, close button, title and buttons (the mail's Open All too; the game menu's buttons are made again each time it opens and are re-dressed each time, its header plate is hidden, and the ForeverUI button on it matches). Friends, the dressing room, the flight map, books and letters (their pages in light text on the dark panel), and the windows that load later (auction house, professions, inspect, guild bank, collections, achievements, dungeon journal) get the same look. Loss of control (stunned, feared...) shows on a flat panel a little above your character, and the talking head on one at the top center (or hidden; your choice under Bars, map, chat). On a Hardcore realm, the game's death notices (the red lines at the top center) take the theme font and the theme's red. If you like (off until you switch it on, under Bars, map, chat), a rare that shows up on your minimap is announced with the raid warning sound and one chat line, out of combat and once per rare. The character pane shows each worn item's durability on its slot, colored like the info strip's. Their own layout and buttons stay as the game has them. Switches under Bars, map, chat.
- **The game's tools**: the game's Cooldown Manager (turn it on under the game's Options, Gameplay Enhancements, or with ForeverUI's switch "Show the game's Cooldown Manager" under Bars, map, chat, off until you tick it, which turns the game's setting on and, unticked, off again; ForeverUI says so once if it's off) gets square icons in thin borders with the theme font, its cooldown timers in the theme's timer font, and its rows sit between the player and target frames, above the swing timer, the essential row nearest them. The game's damage meter gets a flat panel with theme bars and text, above the bag and menu bar in the bottom-right corner (the tooltip corner moves just above it). Move them with `/fui unlock`; while Edit Mode is open, ForeverUI stands aside and puts them back when it closes. One switch leaves placing them to Edit Mode. Switches under Bars, map, chat.
- **Everything else**: XP and reputation bars, square minimap (brass diamonds on its corners, in your accent), chat skin, combined bag (switched on once, not at every login, and given back when you switch the module off; with a Sort button beside the search box where the game can sort bags, replacing the game's own (the search box narrowed so Sort fits inside the window), item slots bordered in each item's quality color, search dimming kept, and the game's search box in the theme; its slots sorted into sections, AdiBags-style: quest items, equipment, consumables, trade goods, other, junk (gray items) and empty slots, each under a header in your accent and side by side where they fit, with a clear gap above each header, the slots, headers, search row and money line all 12 px inside the window's edges, and the window growing to fit them (the game draws a bag too tall for your screen a little smaller, as it does its own); an item the game hasn't loaded yet waits under Other and moves when it has; in combat the bag keeps its layout and catches up when combat ends (opened in combat, it shows the game's plain grid until then); one switch under Bars, map, chat gives back the plain grid at once) and the bank in the same look, and themed tooltips with class colors and a Target line.
- **Fight history** (off until you turn it on under Bars, map, chat): a sparkline of your DPS over the last 20 fights, with a tooltip, under the damage meter. Each fight is one bar, as tall as its share of your best, the latest in your accent color; a fight whose figure the game kept hidden leaves an empty slot. Hovering lists each fight, newest first, with its length, your DPS and, in a group, your place ("#1  1:23  412 dps  #2 of 5"); `/fui fights` prints the same. Fights are kept even while the strip is off, and forgotten when you log out. It has its own visibility (Always, On mouseover, ...) and moves with `/fui unlock`.
- **Strip under the minimap**: durability (orange at 25% or less, red at 10% or less), free bag slots, fps and latency, gold, ammo for hunters, warriors and rogues (soul shards for warlocks, counted across your bags and the reagent bag, red at none), and the time. While you level, a third row adds your XP per hour this session, the time to your next level at that pace, and your gold per hour (red while you're spending); they wait a minute for the numbers to settle, and the XP ones hide at the level cap. A fourth row can show your DPS in the last fight, from the game's damage meter (off until you turn it on; it updates when a fight ends, and stays hidden where the game keeps it secret), and, in a group, your place in that fight's DPS ("#2 of 5", the place the game's own meter shows; also off until you turn it on). Each part can be turned off; one the game can't answer stays hidden. The strip is always shown unless you pick another visibility for it (On mouseover, In combat, Hidden or the action bars' row of conditions); so are the XP and reputation bars, which share one setting. The quest tracker sits just under the strip with room for all four rows.
- **Vendor memory**: tooltips say which merchant sells an item (and where) once you've visited one: "Sold by Thomas Miller (Elwynn Forest, Goldshire)", with "limited stock" when the merchant carries only a few, for up to three merchants per item, the one you saw last first. The Keep stocked list (`/fui stock`) says where to buy each item too. What merchants sell is remembered for every character on the account; `/fui vendors` says how many merchants are remembered and `/fui vendors clear` forgets them. Two switches under Bars, map, chat (Tooltips): the tooltip line, and remembering at all.
- **At a merchant**: repairs with your own gold and sells your gray items (tried again a few times, since the server limits how fast items sell). The error line drops what the action bar already shows (ability not ready, not enough rage or energy, no target); errors you can act on stay, shown by the game's own error line (its sounds and its one line per kind of error, which refreshes instead of stacking). Each can be turned off. Item tooltips show what the stack sells for (unless the game already shows a price) and how many you have, bags and bank, with an optional item ID line.
- **Chat extras**: web addresses in chat become links in your accent color; click one and it opens selected, ready to copy. A small **Copy** button over the chat (or `/fui copy`) opens the recent chat as plain text to copy. Short channel names (off until you switch it on): `[2. Trade - City]` shows as `[T]`, `[Guild]` as `[G]`, `[Raid Leader]` as `[RL]`, and clicking the short tag still opens the channel. Ctrl + mouse wheel jumps to the top or the bottom of a chat window. The game's own chat timestamps and class-colored names can be set under `/fui` → Bars, map, chat → Chat too (they are the game's settings, so the game's options show the same). Chat the game hides from addons (in instances) is left untouched, never shortened, and out of the copy.
- **Chat watch**: phrases that ring a sound and show a toast you can click to whisper the sender. Add phrases ("LFM", "WTS Devilsaur") under Bars, map, chat → Chat watch or with `/fui watch add LFM`, and tick the channels to watch (numbered channels such as Trade and LFG, and yell, by default; say, guild, party and raid, whispers if you like). When a line says one (any case, item links included), the whisper sound plays and a small accent-edged toast at the top of the screen shows the sender and the line for 8 seconds; click it to whisper them, right-click to dismiss. One alert per sender and phrase every 30 seconds, and your own lines never ring. Chat itself is untouched. In dungeons, raids and encounters, where the game hides chat from addons, nothing matches. Up to 20 phrases of 40 characters; the toast moves with `/fui unlock`.
- **Minimap drawer**: other addons' round minimap buttons are gathered into a drawer beside the map; the small "..." button on the map's left edge opens it (it only shows once there's a button to gather).
- **Auto-accept** (each off until you switch it on, under Bars, map, chat): accept summons (out of combat), accept resurrections (the game's prompt closes whichever kind it was), decline duel requests. Each says in chat what it did.
- **Faction look** (on by default; General → Look → Faction look): an Alliance character gets the crossed swords in gold and Cinzel for window titles and headings; a Horde character gets the crossed axes in red and Metamorphous. The crest sits beside the options window's title, on the setup wizard, on the AFK card and faintly behind the quest dialog's header. Numbers and body text stay in Fira Sans, the HUD and your accent color are unchanged, and neutral characters (and Korean and Chinese clients, which use the game's font) keep the usual look; Russian clients keep Fira Sans for titles and headings (the faction fonts have no Cyrillic) and get the crest. `/fui factionart` lists which of the game's own faction pictures your client has.
- **AFK screen**: while you're away (`/afk`, or the game marks you idle), the screen dims to a card with your name in your class color, level and class, the zone, how long you've been away, the time and your XP bar. Moving or a fight brings everything back; a click or a key puts it away until you're next away. It only shows out of combat and never hides the game's own frames. Turn it off under Modules.
- **Main tank frames**: in a raid, the players marked main tank or main assist get small tiles at the top left (beside the raid tools), with health in their class color and what each is targeting written beside it. Click one to target them. Move them with `/fui unlock`; turn them off under Modules.
- **Party pets and targets** (off until you turn it on under Unit frames): a small tile for each party member's pet and target, one row per member, above the party frames. They show only in a party, each while its unit exists.
- **Quest dialog**: talking to a quest giver opens ForeverUI's own dialog just above the frames around your character (clear of the cast bars) instead of the game's quest and gossip windows, sized to what the NPC has to say: the NPC's portrait (a quest mark in your accent color when there is none to draw, such as for a quest started from an item) and name, the text (a long text, or a long list of quests and options, scrolls with the mouse wheel once the dialog reaches its greatest height), numbered lines for quests (in their difficulty colors; gold when ready to turn in) and gossip options, reward cards with quality borders lined up under the text, and the money and XP you'll get. Press a number to pick a line or a reward, Space for the main button (Accept, Continue, Complete), Escape to say goodbye (hiding the whole UI with Alt-Z hides the dialog with it and keeps the conversation open, so it is there when the UI comes back). Movable with `/fui unlock`; switch it off under `/fui` → Quests to get the game's windows back.
- **Quest tracker**: ForeverUI's own, in the tracker's spot under the info strip: each quest you watch as "[12] Title" in its difficulty color, its objectives (gray, then green when done) or "Ready to turn in", and its usable quest item as a button beside it (with its cooldown, a red tint while the target is out of range, and the game's item tooltip on hover). In combat the counts and text keep updating; the item buttons can't move then, so they take their new places when combat ends. Left-click a quest to open it, right-click to stop watching it; collapse it with the arrow; order it as the game does or nearest first (`/fui` → Quests). Quests that don't fit are counted ("+3 more"). The game's tracker is hidden while it shows; switch it off to get the game's back (in ForeverUI's look).
- **Quest log**: the game's quest log key and the quest log button on the bag and menu bar (and `/fui quests`) open ForeverUI's own instead of the game's: your quests under their zones (click a zone to fold it; a long list, and a long quest text, scroll with the mouse wheel), "[12] Title" in its difficulty color with a check when ready to turn in; the chosen quest's text, objectives and reward cards, and Track/Untrack, Share (in a group), Abandon (through the game's own confirmation) and Map. Clicking a quest in the tracker opens it here. Switch it off under `/fui` → Quests to keep the game's.
- **Quest automation** (each off until you switch it on, under `/fui` → Quests; hold Shift while talking to skip it): accept quests, turn in finished ones (it never chooses among rewards for you, unless you also switch on "take the one that sells for most"), and skip the chatter when an NPC has just one quest for you. Each says in chat what it did.
- **Errands**: a **Train all** button on the trainer's window learns everything you can afford in one click (every purchase happens inside the click, as the game wants), and says what it cost. **Keep stocked**: `/fui stock <shift-click an item> 20` keeps 20 in your bags; a merchant who sells it tops you up, a stack at a time and in whole merchant stacks (ammo comes 200 at a time), while you can pay (`/fui stock` lists them, with where to buy each once Vendor memory has seen a merchant selling it; `0` removes one, `clear` empties the list). The **flight timer** saves each flight's time by route, and the next flight on that route shows a bar (movable) counting down to landing. Buying and training stop in combat. Each has a switch under Bars, map, chat.
- **Instance prep**: a card at the door listing what you're short of. Stepping into a dungeon or raid, it lists what is low, each with a red mark and "have / want": ammo (hunters), soul shards (warlocks), your class's reagents for your level (runes and powder, candles, seeds and berries, symbols, ankhs, powders), food and drink, bandages, potions, durability under 50%, and anything on your Keep-stocked list below its count; with nothing short it stays away. A ready check (or `/fui prep`) always shows it, saying "Everything's packed." when nothing is short. It fades after 20 s or on its close button, never checks in combat (`/fui prep` waits for the fight to end), and moves with `/fui unlock`. Under Bars, map, chat: switch the door and ready-check cards, Hide after and the durability warning; `/fui prep want food 30` sets how many to carry.
- **Situations**: record a different layout and settings for raids, PvP, instances or resting; they apply out of combat as you move between them. One situation holds at a time, the first of PvP (a battleground or arena), Instance (a dungeon or raid instance), Raid, Party, Resting and Solo. To set one up, pick **Record for: Raid** on the Situations page (or `/fui situation record raid`), move frames with `/fui unlock` (spot, scale, opacity) and change settings as usual, then **Stop recording and save** (`/fui situation stop`): only what you changed is kept. An override can hold any frame's spot, scale and opacity, the info strip's, menu bar's and XP bars' visibility, the info strip's blocks, and the nameplates' levels, threat colors, target border and quest marks; nothing else is recorded. Leaving the situation puts your own layout back, and your profile is always saved and exported with your own values. A change to something the active override sets lasts until you leave the situation. The page says which situation you're in and what its override changes; switch them all off there, or clear the active one's. Profiles carry their overrides (an export string has them too, as overrides).
- **Auction helper**: at the auction house, a ForeverUI panel docks to the left of the game's window. **Shop**: named shopping lists (pick one, or make one, in the dropdown; **+ Add** takes an item link, an item ID or a name; up to 20 lists of 50 items, kept for the account); **Search list** searches each item in turn (one search at a time, at most one a second, so well under the game's limit; "12 of 50" says how far it is), and each row shows the item's cheapest price per unit and how many are up ("none up" when nothing is). Click a row and the game's own list shows that item, to buy there; right-click a row to take it off the list; the mouse wheel scrolls the rows. **Sell**: drop an item on the card (or pick it in the game's sell tab, as you would for the game) and it shows the cheapest price someone else asks now (your own listings left out), the lowest of the last 7 days from your scans and searches, the deposit for the game's auction length, and the price it will ask: 1 silver under the cheapest (or 1 copper, 1 %, or the same price; never under 1 silver for the silver rule; in whole silver where the game takes no copper), else under the 7-day low when nothing is up. **Post** lists it at that price, a commodity's stack at a unit price, any other item one at a time; it waits while the game is busy, and a price the game wants you to confirm is left to the game's own Sell tab. **Your auctions**: **Check undercuts** asks for your auctions, checks each item's price now, and marks with a red square each auction someone else asks less for per unit; each row has a **Cancel** button (hover it for what cancelling costs), and only a click on it cancels. A line under the rows then says how many are undercut ("3 undercut", or "none undercut"), and **Cancel next undercut** cancels the first of them on each click, one auction per click, and moves to the next (hover it for which one and what cancelling costs); with none left it says so and stops. A mark stays until the next check. Each row also says how the auction stands: still up (your price per unit, with Cancel), sold ("sold · 7s 50c", what it sold for, in green), or expired ("expired · by mail" while the item comes back by mail); once the item is back in your bags it says "expired · in bags", and its **Relist** button puts the item on the sell card, its price checked as if you had dropped it there. The rows stay while the auction house is closed, so an expired one is still there when you come back from the mailbox. If you like (off until you switch it on), your auctions are checked again after each post and each cancel, one search at a time like any other. **Scan the auction house** (one scan every 15 minutes, the game's own limit, counted from the start of a scan that finished: one cut short by closing the auction house doesn't count; the line under the button says when the last one was) reads every listing, a few hundred per frame so the game never stalls, and records, leaving your own listings out (the chat line says how many were yours), each item's cheapest price per unit, how many listings and units are up, and its market value (the average price of the cheapest 15 % of the units, so one cheap listing doesn't set it and a few dear ones don't lift it); from then on item tooltips everywhere say "AH: 1s 0c · market 1s 50c (today)" (the market left out when it's the cheapest price), and over a stack a second line says what the stack comes to at both ("AH x20: 20s 0c · market 30s 0c"). Searches feed the prices between scans: the shop's list searches, the game's own browse list, the sell card's price check and Check undercuts each record the cheapest price someone else asks (your own listings left out; from the browse list only for items it prices per unit, commodities and equipment), while the units up and the market value come only from a full scan. Prices are kept per realm and faction, since each has its own auction house (every character of yours on that realm and side shares them; prices saved by an older version move once to the realm and faction you next log in with), with each day's low, units up and market value for 30 days; one not seen for 30 days is forgotten, and so is a realm and faction left with none. Shopping lists are kept for the whole account. `/fui ah` says how many items are priced on this realm and faction and when you last scanned there, `/fui ah list <name>` picks or makes a list and `/fui ah add <list> <item>` adds to one, `/fui ah scan` scans and `/fui ah prices clear` forgets them (on every realm). Under Bars, map, chat (Auction house): the panel, the tooltip line and the scan button each have a switch, the undercut rule is there too (and on the card), and so is checking your auctions again after each post and cancel. Buying and bidding stay in the game's own window.
- **Options**: `/fui`, the addons button, or the **ForeverUI** button at the top of the game menu (Escape). When ForeverUI has caught errors this session, the addons button carries a small red count and the options window's footer says how many (click that line to list them in chat, as `/fui errors` does); with none, or after `/fui errors clear`, there is no badge. Turn any module off under Modules to keep the default one. A setting changed from its default gets a small accent dot by its caption (click it to put the default back), and a page holding any such setting gets one in the list on the left. With the window open, Up and Down go through the pages and Tab (Shift-Tab back) through a page's settings, Enter or Space pressing the one marked; other keys still reach the game, and nothing is taken while you type in the search or a box, or in a fight. The top of General shows a unit frame and an action button in your accent, font, text size and bar texture as you change them. Most changes apply at once; the rest show a Reload now button. WoW Forever lets an addon reload only from a click on a secure button, so that's what ForeverUI's Reload buttons are; one built during a fight becomes one when the fight ends, and until then it asks you to type `/reload`.
- **First-run setup**: the first time you log in on an account, a four-step wizard asks for a layout (Damage and tanking, Healing, or Like the game's), sizes for your screen, an accent (color swatches) and a raid frame style (three small raid tiles, each drawn in its style in your class color), then unlocks your frames so you can move them (the same unlock mode as `/fui unlock`, or **Move frames** under General in `/fui`; `/fui lock` or Done locks them again). With the defaults it takes four clicks: Next, Next, Next, Done. The screen step offers 1080p, 1440p, 4K and ultrawide presets and picks the one for your screen (or Keep my current sizes, picked on a screen under 1000 pixels tall): each sets the game's UI scale (1080p 80%, 1440p and ultrawide 70%, 4K 65%, the bottom of the game's own range; it sizes the unit frames, which every layout draws in UI units), the action button size (38 px, the default), the raid tile size (100 x 48, or 110 x 48 on the bigger screens; it shows after a reload, which setup offers) and the text size (100% to 110%). The UI scale is the same setting as the game's Options → Graphics → UI Scale, applied out of combat; a preset turns ForeverUI's pixel-perfect scale off, since that would replace it. It waits until you're out of combat. Done, the close button and Escape all end it, and it doesn't come back on its own; `/fui setup` reopens it. Damage and tanking is ForeverUI's defaults (picking it puts back anything another layout moved); Like the game's puts your player and target frames along the top left as the game has them (the pet under the player, the target of target and focus beside the target), the party and raid down the left side, the boss frames on the right under your buffs, and the raid tools and main tanks near the top center; Healing puts the raid frames centered just above your cast bar, growing upward, and turns click casting on. With Healing, a party and raids of up to 2 groups fit between the player and target frames; raids of more than 2 groups overlap them, so drag the raid frames where you want them with `/fui unlock` (or **Move frames** under General in `/fui`).

Every client call is checked before use, and values the client hides from
addons go to the bars unread. Auras use the client's own aura containers where
it has them (12.1 and WoW Forever), so icons, stacks, timers and debuff colors
keep working while it hides aura values in instanced combat; if the client
refuses one of those containers, ForeverUI switches to its own icons for the
rest of the session (it says so in `/fui errors`); on those icons, an aura
filter word the client doesn't know (say crowd control) is dropped for the
session, and it is listed in `/fui errors`; `/fui status` shows which
aura backend is in use and which optional client calls were found. If the client can't compile secure snippets (a WoW Forever beta bug), ForeverUI says so at login and in `/fui status` and turns hover keys off; mouse click-casting still works, bar 1 still pages with stances, forms and Shift+number (through the game's own page attribute rather than a snippet), and the raid and main tank tiles are set up without one. None of this has been checked in game yet:
please report what breaks.

## Install

Download `ForeverUI-<version>.zip` from the [latest release](https://github.com/clifftaylor2325/wow-forever-ui/releases/latest) and unzip the `ForeverUI` folder into `World of Warcraft/_retail_/Interface/AddOns/` (your Forever client's AddOns folder).

## Commands

```
/fui               the options window (also: left-click the addons button by the minimap)
/fui setup         the first-run setup again: layout, screen, look, then move things
/fui unlock | lock drag handles on every ForeverUI frame (also: right-click the addons button)
/fui reset <frame|all>
/fui accent <rrggbb|brass|arcane|fel|void|ember> | reset      /fui theme reset
/fui modules       the list of modules; /fui enable|disable <module>, then /reload
/fui skins         addons skinned through the API; skins <addon> on|off
/fui profile [name]              switch (or create); profile new|copy|delete <name>, profile reset
/fui export [module] | import    profile strings (whole profile or one module's settings)
/fui spec [profile|off]          use a profile whenever you're in this specialization
/fui binds                       this spec's click-casting and hover-key bindings
/fui keybind                     keybind mode: hover an action button and press a key to bind it; Escape leaves
/fui fights                      your DPS, length and place in each fight this session, newest first
/fui vendors [clear]             how many merchants ForeverUI remembers (for tooltips and the stock list); clear forgets them
/fui prep [want <kind> <n>]      the Instance prep card now; want sets how many food, bandages, potions, ammo or shards to carry
/fui watch [add|remove <phrase>|list|clear]   Chat watch's phrases: add one, remove one, list them, or clear them all
/fui situation [record <name>|stop|clear [name]|list]   Situations: record an override for solo, party, raid, pvp, instance or resting, stop and save it, clear one (the active one without a name), or list them
/fui ah [list <name>|add <list> <item>|scan|prices clear]   auction prices from your scans: how many and when; pick or make a shopping list and say what's on it; add an item (a link, an ID or a name) to a list; scan the auction house (while it's open); forget the prices
/fui status
/fui errors [clear]              what ForeverUI caught going wrong this session: the version, client and aura backend, then each distinct error once with how often it happened and its stack (paste it in a bug report; clear empties the list). An error is printed to chat the first time it happens and handed to the game's error popup or your error addon every time; an error in one module, skin, event handler or after-combat update is kept here and doesn't stop the others
/fui perf [cpu|watch]            what ForeverUI costs: its CPU time (in all, and since the last report) and memory now, and its ten busiest functions; cpu turns the game's script profiling on or off (after a reload; it slows every addon a little, so turn it off when done); watch prints the line every minute until asked again
```

To measure ForeverUI's CPU cost yourself: `/fui perf cpu`, reload, play a while (a raid pull is the hard case), then `/fui perf`; `/fui perf cpu` again (and a reload) turns profiling off.

## Skin API (for other addons)

```
## OptionalDeps: ForeverUI
```
```lua
if ForeverUI and ForeverUI.RegisterSkin then
  ForeverUI.RegisterSkin("MyAddon", function(S)
    S.Panel(MyFrame, { shadow = true }) -- flat panel, 1px border, sheen; shadow for floating windows
    S.Button(MyFrame.OkButton)
    S.EditBox(MyFrame.Input)
    S.CloseButton(MyFrame.Close)
    S.Text(MyFrame.Title, "title")  -- text roles: title, heading, name, cell, label, number, small, tiny, world
    S.Color(MyFrame.Hint, "text2")  -- theme colors by name: text, text2, muted, accent, good, bad...
    S.Tint(MyFrame.Glow, "accent")
  end)
end
```

The callback runs once at login, or immediately if the addon registers after login. Errors in it are reported and don't break anything else. Painted frames follow `/fui accent` changes live, and their borders stay one screen pixel at any UI scale (`S.Pixel(frame)` gives that size). `S.RGB(name)` returns any theme color; the names, text roles and the rest of the look are listed in `docs/THEME.md` (from `Core/Theme.lua`). `S.apiVersion` is 1; the API only grows.

## Development

```bash
npm install
npm test          # JS and Lua harness tests under Vitest, once (vitest run, no watch), then a per-area Lua harness summary
npm run verify    # npm test (real addon Lua in a TEST-ONLY headless WoW client, and the JS tests), then the code-quality scan; CI runs this
                  # (functions of 10 lines or fewer, all documented; colors only from Core/Theme.lua)
npm run coverage  # line coverage of the addon Lua; fails below scripts/lua-coverage-floor.json
npm run lint      # luacheck
npm run media     # regenerate Media/Textures and Media/Icons from scripts/lib/media-sources.mjs
npm run package   # dist/ForeverUI-<version>.zip from committed files
npm run preview   # preview/*.png: the default layout and the options window (needs Playwright)
```

**Play from your clone:** `npm run link:wow -- --wow "<Forever folder that has Interface/AddOns>"` links `Interface/AddOns/ForeverUI` to this clone (a directory junction on Windows, no admin needed), so an edit plus `/reload` shows in game. The folder is remembered in `~/.wow-forever/config.json`, so after the first time it's just `npm run link:wow`. An installed copy is only moved aside with `--replace` (it becomes `ForeverUI.backup-<date>`). `--status` shows what's there; `--unlink` removes the link and never deletes a real folder.

`docs/SMOKE-TEST.md` is the in-game checklist to run after a release lands: what to do, what to expect and what to paste back for each step.

**VS Code:** open the folder and accept the recommended extensions: **Lua** (sumneko) and **WoW API** (Ketho), which add Lua 5.1 checking plus autocomplete and docs for the game API. `.vscode/tasks.json` has verify, luacheck (problems show in the editor), coverage, package, preview and link:wow under *Run Task*.

**Windows tools:** `npm run verify` needs only Node. For `npm run lint`, put `luacheck.exe` from the [luacheck releases](https://github.com/lunarmodules/luacheck/releases) on your PATH. CI also runs a Lua 5.1 syntax check (`luac5.1 -p`); you don't need it locally.

**Release:** bump `## Version` in `ForeverUI.toc` and merge, then run the `release` workflow from the Actions tab on `main` (it tags `v<version>`), or push a matching tag. It re-runs the checks and publishes a GitHub release with the zip.

**Art and fonts:** every texture and icon under `Media/` is drawn by code in `scripts/lib/media-sources.mjs` (plain math, no image editor), and `npm run verify` fails if a committed file doesn't match its source. The fonts are made from upstream Fira Sans Condensed by `scripts/fonts/make-fonts.py` (see that file).

**Testing the addon:** `scripts/tests/<area>.test.mjs` play the addon's real Lua in a headless client built for the tests (`scripts/lib/wow-*.lua`): Lua 5.1 semantics, frames with real layout on a 1920x1080 screen, a world model (units, auras, actions, quests, combat, timers) behind the game API, and mouse and keyboard input. A test sets up the world, acts as the player would, and asserts on what the addon drew. The guide is [scripts/tests/README.md](scripts/tests/README.md).

**Code quality:** every function has a comment on the line above it and at most 10 lines of body code; `npm run verify` and CI enforce it. The shared libraries in `Libs/` stay exactly as upstream wrote them, so the scan, luacheck and the coverage count leave them out.
